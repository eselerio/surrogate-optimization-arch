\section{Scope and coordinate contract}

This supplement completes the mechanistic generator, data design, five-fold cross-validation, ridge estimator, physical-projection quadratic program (QP), exact-QP active-set surrogate optimization, smooth mechanistic nonlinear program (NLP), reference replay, and reporting rules. The structural equations use $N$ reactors, $L$ Clarifier layers, $F=F_S+F_X$ components, and $K$ independent invariants. Their dimensions are
\begin{equation}
n_\chi=(N+3)F+L,\qquad n_y=NF+L,\qquad
n_h=2F+NK+F_S+2,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
n_g=F_X+2(L-2),\qquad n_q=n_\chi+n_g.
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
n_S=d_\vartheta,\qquad
n_M=d_\vartheta+NF+L+1,\qquad
n_{M,=}=NF+L+1+n_{\mathcal V,=}.
\end{equation}
Here $n_\chi$ is the complete surrogate-response dimension, $n_y$ is the reduced mechanistic-state dimension, $n_h$ counts projection equalities, $n_g$ counts physical-direction inequalities, and $n_q$ includes response non-negativity. The production surrogate outer problem contains only the $d_\vartheta$ controls; a separate exact QP supplies the projected state and its active-set sensitivities at each trial. The direct NLP adds one feed-TSS auxiliary to its controls and reduced state; $n_{\mathcal V,=}$ counts any stage-allocation equalities in its operating set. These expressions, rather than their numerical case-study values, govern the formulation.

Every component vector uses the order
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
(&S_O,S_F,S_A,S_{NH4},S_{NO2},S_{NO3},S_{N2},S_{PO4},S_I,S_{ALK},\\
&X_I,X_S,X_H,X_{PAO},X_{PP},X_{PHA},X_{AOB},X_{NOB},X_{MeP},X_{MeOH}).
\end{split}
\label{eq:Sorder}
\end{equation}
Define the particulate index set $\mathcal X$ as the component indices selected by $E_X$. For the case study, $F=20$, the first ten entries are soluble, and the final ten are particulate. Soluble oxygen, COD, nitrogen, phosphorus, and particulate coordinates retain their stated ASM mass bases; alkalinity is mol HCO$_3^-$ m$^{-3}$.

\section{Complete mechanistic generator}

\subsection{Generic stage balance and case specialization}

For reactor $i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$, define the fresh-flow-basis stage residence time $h_i=24V_i/Q_0$ h. Before division by volume, the component balance is
\begin{equation}
V_i\dot c_i=Q_0q_P(c_{i-1}-c_i)
+V_i\{\nu^\top\rho(c_i)+\omega_i e_{S_O}+\xi_i\},
\qquad c_0=m.
\label{eq:Sdynamicstage}
\end{equation}
Dividing by $V_i$ gives the dilution rate $d_i=Q_0q_P/V_i=24q_P/h_i$. A negligible-carrier-flow material control enters through $\xi_i$, and its invariant contribution is $A\xi_i/d_i$. If a dosing stream carries non-negligible liquid flow, its flow and composition must instead be added to the mixer or stage hydraulic balance and every downstream dilution rate must be recomputed. The case study uses equal reactor volumes, $h_i=H/5$, no material addition, $\xi_i=0$, and independently controlled aeration in stages 3--5. Consequently $d_i=120q_P/H$ d$^{-1}$ only in this case.

\subsection{Reactor rates}

The kinetic structure uses ASM2d as its base and adds the study-specific nitrite, metal, and boundary-preserving extensions defined completely below \citep{Henze2006}. For non-negative $z$ and positive $K$, define
\begin{equation}
M(z;K)=\frac{z}{K+z},\qquad \overline M(z;K)=\frac{K}{K+z}.
\end{equation}
Let $S_{NOx}=S_{NO2}+S_{NO3}$ and
\begin{equation}
\alpha_2=\frac{S_{NO2}}{S_{NOx}},\quad \alpha_3=\frac{S_{NO3}}{S_{NOx}},\quad
\alpha_F=\frac{S_F}{S_F+S_A},\quad \alpha_A=\frac{S_A}{S_F+S_A}.
\end{equation}
A ratio is defined as zero when its denominator is zero. Also define
\begin{equation}
\theta_X=\frac{X_S}{K_XX_H+X_S},\quad r_{PP}=\frac{X_{PP}}{X_{PAO}},\quad
r_{PHA}=\frac{X_{PHA}}{X_{PAO}},
\end{equation}
with the same zero-denominator convention, and
\begin{equation}
\psi_{PP}=M(r_{PP};K_{PP}),\quad \psi_{PHA}=M(r_{PHA};K_{PHA}),\quad
c_{PP}=\frac{\pos{K_{\max}-r_{PP}}}{K_{IPP}+\pos{K_{\max}-r_{PP}}}.
\end{equation}
All PAO rates multiplying $X_{PAO}$ are zero when $X_{PAO}=0$. Repeated nutrient limitations are
\begin{align}
\Lambda_H&=M(S_{NH4};K_{NH4,H})M(S_{PO4};K_{PO4,H})M(S_{ALK};K_{ALK,H}),\\
\Lambda_P&=M(S_{NH4};K_{NH4,PAO})M(S_{PO4};K_{PO4,PAO})M(S_{ALK};K_{ALK,PAO}),\\
\Lambda_N&=M(S_{PO4};K_{PO4,nit})M(S_{ALK};K_{ALK,nit}),\\
\Lambda_{NOB}&=\Lambda_NM(S_{NH4};K_{NH4,NOB}),\qquad
\Lambda_{PRE}=M(S_{ALK};K_{ALK,PRE}).
\end{align}
The extra ammonium factor in $\Lambda_{NOB}$ supplies nitrogen needed for new NOB biomass, while $\Lambda_{PRE}$ prevents precipitation from consuming alkalinity at the zero-alkalinity boundary. Both are required for the non-negative concentration orthant to be forward invariant.

The rate list follows the physical sequence of the model. Rates 1--4 hydrolyze slowly biodegradable substrate under aerobic, nitrite, nitrate, and fermentative conditions. Rates 5--10 describe aerobic and anoxic heterotrophic growth on fermentable substrate or acetate; the fractions $\alpha_F$, $\alpha_A$, $\alpha_2$, and $\alpha_3$ prevent double counting when substrates or electron acceptors coexist. Rate 11 ferments $S_F$ to $S_A$, and rate 12 is heterotrophic decay. Rates 13--22 describe PAO storage, polyphosphate formation, PAO growth, and decay. Rates 23--26 describe AOB/NOB growth and decay, while rates 27--28 precipitate and redissolve phosphorus with the metal phases. With that process map in mind, the 28 non-negative rates are
\begingroup\small
\begin{align}

\rho_2&=\eta_{hyd,NO2}K_H\overline M(S_O;K_{O,hyd})M(S_{NO2};K_{NO2,hyd})\alpha_2\theta_XX_H,\\
\rho_3&=\eta_{hyd,NO3}K_H\overline M(S_O;K_{O,hyd})M(S_{NO3};K_{NO3,hyd})\alpha_3\theta_XX_H,\\

\rho_5&=\mu_HM(S_O;K_{O,H})M(S_F;K_F)\alpha_F\Lambda_HX_H,\\
\rho_6&=\mu_HM(S_O;K_{O,H})M(S_A;K_A)\alpha_A\Lambda_HX_H,\\
\rho_7&=\mu_H\overline M(S_O;K_{O,H})M(S_F;K_F)\alpha_F\Lambda_H\eta_{H,NO3}M(S_{NO3};K_{NO3,H})\alpha_3X_H,\\
\rho_8&=\mu_H\overline M(S_O;K_{O,H})M(S_F;K_F)\alpha_F\Lambda_H\eta_{H,NO2}M(S_{NO2};K_{NO2,H})\alpha_2X_H,\\
\rho_9&=\mu_H\overline M(S_O;K_{O,H})M(S_A;K_A)\alpha_A\Lambda_H\eta_{H,NO3}M(S_{NO3};K_{NO3,H})\alpha_3X_H,\\
\rho_{10}&=\mu_H\overline M(S_O;K_{O,H})M(S_A;K_A)\alpha_A\Lambda_H\eta_{H,NO2}M(S_{NO2};K_{NO2,H})\alpha_2X_H,\\
\rho_{11}&=q_{fe}\overline M(S_O;K_{O,H})\overline M(S_{NOx};K_{NOx,H})M(S_F;K_{fe})M(S_{ALK};K_{ALK,H})X_H,\\
\rho_{12}&=b_HX_H,\\
\rho_{13}&=q_{PHA}M(S_A;K_A)\overline M(S_O;K_{O,PAO})\overline M(S_{NOx};K_{NOx,PAO})M(S_{ALK};K_{ALK,PAO})\psi_{PP}X_{PAO},\\
\rho_{14}&=q_{PP}M(S_O;K_{O,PAO})M(S_{PO4};K_{PS})M(S_{ALK};K_{ALK,PAO})\psi_{PHA}c_{PP}X_{PAO},\\
\rho_{15}&=q_{PP}\overline M(S_O;K_{O,PAO})M(S_{PO4};K_{PS})M(S_{ALK};K_{ALK,PAO})\psi_{PHA}c_{PP}\eta_{PAO,NO3}M(S_{NO3};K_{NO3,PAO})\alpha_3X_{PAO},\\

\rho_{17}&=\mu_{PAO}M(S_O;K_{O,PAO})\Lambda_P\psi_{PHA}X_{PAO},\\
\rho_{18}&=\mu_{PAO}\overline M(S_O;K_{O,PAO})\Lambda_P\psi_{PHA}\eta_{PAO,NO3}M(S_{NO3};K_{NO3,PAO})\alpha_3X_{PAO},\\
\rho_{19}&=\mu_{PAO}\overline M(S_O;K_{O,PAO})\Lambda_P\psi_{PHA}\eta_{PAO,NO2}M(S_{NO2};K_{NO2,PAO})\alpha_2X_{PAO},\\
\rho_{20}&=b_{PAO}M(S_{ALK};K_{ALK,PAO})X_{PAO},\\
\rho_{21}&=b_{PP}M(S_{ALK};K_{ALK,PAO})X_{PP},\qquad
\rho_{22}=b_{PHA}M(S_{ALK};K_{ALK,PAO})X_{PHA},\\

\rho_{25}&=b_{AOB}X_{AOB},\qquad \rho_{26}=b_{NOB}X_{NOB},\\
\rho_{27}&=k_{PRE}S_{PO4}X_{MeOH}\Lambda_{PRE},\\

\end{align}
\endgroup
Rates 1--13, 17--20, and 22--26 use a COD basis; rates 14--16, 21, and 27--28 use a phosphorus basis. The stoichiometric columns below convert each rate basis into changes in all $F$ components.

Aeration is an independent stage setting, not a plant-wide scalar. In the general model,
\begin{equation}
\omega_i=k_La_i(a_i)(S_O^*-c_{i,S_O}).
\label{eq:Soxygen}
\end{equation}
The case study sets $S_O^*=8.5$ g O$_2$ m$^{-3}$, $k_La_1=k_La_2=0$, and $k_La_i=47a_i$ d$^{-1}$ for $i=3,4,5$, with $a_3,a_4,a_5$ varied independently on $[0,1]$.

\end{equation}

\end{equation}

\subsection{Clarifier model}

The Clarifier is divided into $L\ge3$ finite volumes of volumes $V_{{\rm cl},\ell}$ and is fed into layer $\ell_F\in\{1,\ldots,L\}$. Denote feed TSS by
\begin{equation}
X_F=t_X^\top c_N.
\end{equation}
The Tak\'acs double-exponential law first calculates the raw unhindered-minus-flocculent settling tendency
\begin{equation}
v_{\rm raw}(s;X_F)=v_0\!\left[
e^{-r_h(s-f_{ns}X_F)}-e^{-r_p(s-f_{ns}X_F)}\right].
\label{eq:Srawsettling}
\end{equation}
The physical velocity then clips that tendency to $[0,v_0^{\max}]$ \citep{Takacs1991}:
\begin{equation}
v_s(s;X_F)=\max\{0,\min[v_0^{\max},v_{\rm raw}(s;X_F)]\}.
\end{equation}
The gravitational solids flux is $j_s(s;X_F)=s v_s(s;X_F)$. At the interface between an upper layer $\ell$ and its lower receiving layer $\ell+1$, a concentrated receiver can limit the flux. Thus
\begin{equation}
j^{\rm set}_{\ell+1/2}=\begin{cases}
j_s(s_\ell;X_F),&s_{\ell+1}\le X_t,\\
\min\{j_s(s_\ell;X_F),j_s(s_{\ell+1};X_F)\},&s_{\ell+1}>X_t,
\end{cases}
\quad \ell=1,\ldots,L-1.
\end{equation}
Taking downward flux as positive, the upward liquid above the feed contributes a negative advective flux and the underflow below the feed contributes a positive one:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal J_{\ell+1/2}=\begin{cases}
-v_Es_{\ell+1}+j^{\rm set}_{\ell+1/2},&\ell<\ell_F,\\
v_Us_\ell+j^{\rm set}_{\ell+1/2},&\ell\ge\ell_F,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $v_E=Q_0q_E/A_{\rm cl}$ and $v_U=Q_0q_U/A_{\rm cl}$. Boundary fluxes are $\mathcal J_{1/2}=-v_Es_1$ and $\mathcal J_{L+1/2}=v_Us_L$. Accumulation equals incoming minus outgoing interface flux, with the feed source added only to layer $\ell_F$:
\begin{equation}
V_{{\rm cl},\ell}\dot s_\ell=A_{\rm cl}(\mathcal J_{\ell-1/2}-\mathcal J_{\ell+1/2})
+\mathbf1_{\{\ell=\ell_F\}}Q_0q_CX_F,
\quad \ell=1,\ldots,L.
\label{eq:Slayerbalance}
\end{equation}
Summing Equation~\eqref{eq:Slayerbalance} over all layers cancels every internal interface and recovers $q_CX_F=q_Es_1+q_Us_L$ at steady state.

Solubles follow the liquid unchanged. The reduced aggregate-solids model assumes a common particulate composition equal to the steady Clarifier feed composition. The complete component vector reconstructed in layer $\ell$ is therefore
\begin{equation}
c_{{\rm cl},\ell}=E_S^\top E_Sc_N+
E_X^\top\left(\frac{s_\ell}{X_F}E_Xc_N\right),
\label{eq:Scommoncomposition}
\end{equation}
on the declared domain $X_F\ge X_F^{\min}>0$. Thus
\begin{equation}
c_E=c_{{\rm cl},1},\quad c_U=c_{{\rm cl},L},\quad
g_E=q_Ec_E,\quad g_U=q_Uc_U,\quad
m=\frac{x+r_Ic_N+r_Rc_U}{q_P}.
\label{eq:Sreconstruction}
\end{equation}
In particular, $E_Sc_E=E_Sc_U=E_Sc_N$, while $E_Xc_E=(s_1/X_F)E_Xc_N$ and $E_Xc_U=(s_L/X_F)E_Xc_N$. Equations~\eqref{eq:Scommoncomposition}--\eqref{eq:Sreconstruction}, together with $y$, define the reference response $\chi(\vartheta,x,y)$. In the smooth NLP, every occurrence of $X_F$ in this reconstruction and in the Clarifier residual is replaced by the bounded auxiliary $\widetilde X_F$, with the equality $\widetilde X_F=t_X^\top c_N$ imposed separately. The construction conserves every particulate component at steady state because the summed TSS balance multiplies the common feed fraction. During pseudo-transient relaxation the feed fractions change algebraically; individual particulate layer inventories are therefore not physical dynamic states. Stability and replay statements are explicitly limited to this reduced steady-state model.

\subsection{Steady-state generation and acceptance}

The reduced state is
\begin{equation}
y=\col(c_1,\ldots,c_N,s_1,\ldots,s_L)\in\R_+^{NF+L}.
\end{equation}
At each residual evaluation, the common-composition Clarifier outlet states are reconstructed first, then the mixer, and finally the $N$ reactor and $L$ layer residuals. Variable-order BDF is used as a pseudo-transient route to the nonsmooth steady equations. In the case study its maximum horizon is $\max(400\ \mathrm{d},(50/w)\ \mathrm{d})$, and its maximum step is one hundredth of that horizon. Relative and dimensionless absolute tolerances are $10^{-7}$ and $10^{-9}$. Every route is integrated in log coordinates from $y_{j,+}^{(0)}=\max\{y_j^{(0)},10^{-8}\operatorname{unit}(y_j)\}$:
\begin{equation}
y_j^{\log}=\log\!\left[\frac{y_j}{\operatorname{unit}(y_j)}\right],\qquad
y_j=\operatorname{unit}(y_j)e^{y_j^{\log}},\qquad
\dot y_j^{\log}=f_{{\rm ref},j}(\vartheta,x,y)/y_j,
\label{eq:Slogstate}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{unit}(y_j)$ means one unit in the physical unit assigned to coordinate $j$. This transformation keeps every BDF trial state inside the positive domain, keeps the common-composition denominator defined, and leaves every strictly positive steady root unchanged. A route that cannot pass the endpoint checks is rejected. A remaining endpoint may receive one domain-restricted non-negative least-squares polish with at most 5,000 residual evaluations and $10^{-9}$ step, objective, and gradient tolerances; its line search rejects a trial before residual evaluation whenever $t_X^\top c_N<X_F^{\min}$.

The two case-study initializations depend deterministically on the influent rather than being undocumented fixed vectors. Start 1 sets $c_i^{(0)}=x$ and $s_\ell^{(0)}=t_X^\top x$. Start 2 sets $E_Sc_i^{(0)}=E_Sx$, multiplies every particulate influent coordinate in stages 1--5 by $(1.5,2.0,2.5,3.0,3.5)$, respectively, and multiplies the resulting final-reactor TSS by the layer factors
\begin{equation}
(0.002,0.005,0.01,0.03,0.10,0.50,1.25,2.25,3.25,4.00).
\end{equation}

Define the diagonal generation scale $D_{y,{\rm gen}}$ coordinatewise as the larger of one unit and the declared influent upper bound for a reactor coordinate, or the larger of 1 g TSS m$^{-3}$ and feed TSS for a layer coordinate. Every heterogeneous equality is audited termwise: if row $a$ is $\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_a}t=0$, its dimensionless residual is
\begin{equation}
r_a^{\rm bal}=\frac{|\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_a}t|}
{\max\{\operatorname{unit}_a,\max_{t\in\mathcal T_a}|t|\}},
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{unit}_a$ is one unit in row $a$'s physical units. State and rate negativity are likewise divided by one unit of the corresponding coordinate or rate. An accepted target requires the maximum reactor, layer, component-closure, outlet-identity, and external-invariant $r_a^{\rm bal}$ to be no greater than $10^{-8}$; scaled state negativity no greater than $10^{-10}$; scaled rate negativity no greater than $10^{-12}$; a valid layer envelope; $X_F\ge1$ g TSS m$^{-3}$; and external solids loss $\dot M_X/Q_0\ge1$ g TSS m$^{-3}$. Thus no numerical tolerance compares quantities having different units.

Let $f_{\rm ref}(\vartheta,x,y)$ denote the physical right-hand side of the nonsmooth reduced pseudo-transient model. The stability matrix is the correctly scaled similarity transform
\begin{equation}
J_z=D_{y,{\rm gen}}^{-1}\frac{\partial f_{\rm ref}}{\partial y}D_{y,{\rm gen}}.
\label{eq:Sscaledjacobian}
\end{equation}
Let $y_{\rm sc}=D_{y,{\rm gen}}^{-1}y$ and $\mathfrak f_{\rm stab}(y_{\rm sc})=D_{y,{\rm gen}}^{-1}f_{\rm ref}(\vartheta,x,D_{y,{\rm gen}}y_{\rm sc})$. For column $j$, a centered difference is used only when $y_{{\rm sc},j}>2\delta_{\rm FD}$; otherwise the domain-respecting second-order forward formula
\begin{equation}
\frac{-3\mathfrak f_{\rm stab}(y_{\rm sc})
+4\mathfrak f_{\rm stab}(y_{\rm sc}+\delta_{\rm FD} e_j)
-\mathfrak f_{\rm stab}(y_{\rm sc}+2\delta_{\rm FD} e_j)}{2\delta_{\rm FD}}
\label{eq:Sdomainfd}
\end{equation}
is used. Complete matrices are formed at $\delta_{\rm FD}=\epsilon_{\rm mach}^{1/3}$ and $2\delta_{\rm FD}$. For an interior differentiable point, Equation~\eqref{eq:Sscaledjacobian} is similar to $\partial f_{\rm ref}/\partial y$ and therefore has the same eigenvalues; scaling changes conditioning, not the physical stability classification. At an active non-negativity face, Equation~\eqref{eq:Sdomainfd} is explicitly a feasible-side reduced-model diagnostic, not an unconstrained spectral theorem. The estimate is accepted only if the two rightmost-real-part values differ by at most $10^{-6}$ d$^{-1}$ and their larger value is no greater than $-10^{-8}$ d$^{-1}$. This agreement tolerance was frozen before the article-scale run after the complete 500-input implementation preflight: 21 endpoints that passed every balance, non-negativity, two-start, branch, and stability-margin check exceeded the originally proposed $10^{-9}$ d$^{-1}$ agreement, while the largest observed two-step discrepancy was $9.281\times10^{-7}$ d$^{-1}$. The revised value treats the comparison as a finite-difference consistency diagnostic and does not relax the strictly negative stability margin.

Candidate generation uses the executable branch rule applied by the nonsmooth reference solver: receiver, settling positive-part/floor, velocity-cap, receiver-flux-minimum, and kinetic storage-capacity classifications are recorded for each endpoint, and the two starts must return exactly the same classification tuple. No development-calibrated branch-margin screen is imposed during candidate acceptance. A tie or roundoff-level classification disagreement is therefore an explicitly rejected attempt and triggers a deterministic replacement under the sampling contract below. The scale-dependent quantities in Equations~\eqref{eq:Ssmoothkineticshares}--\eqref{eq:Ssmoothscales} are retained for the smooth direct NLP and for tagging branch-boundary ambiguity at selected-candidate replay; they are not retroactively used to alter the accepted generation set. This corrects the earlier draft's unimplemented two-pass branch-margin description while preserving the generator's frozen, order-independent acceptance rule.

Both initializations are run for every design point. Each route must independently pass the acceptance tests, and the two accepted roots must satisfy
\begin{equation}
\|D_{y,{\rm gen}}^{-1}(y^{(1)}-y^{(2)})\|_\infty\le10^{-6}
\end{equation}
with identical receiver, settling, and kinetic storage-capacity branch classifications. If only one route succeeds, two distinct stable roots are found, or the branch tuples disagree, the candidate is labeled unresolved, retained in the attempt ledger, excluded from the accepted block, and replaced by the next candidate prescribed below. This order-independent rule prevents the accepted training response from depending on which initial condition happened to be tried first.

Finally, every non-negative coordinate face is audited through the inward-pointing condition, with $\mathbf e_j^{(y)}$ denoting reduced-state coordinate $j$,
\begin{equation}
(\mathbf e_j^{(y)})^\top f_{\rm ref}(\vartheta,x,y)\big|_{y_j=0}\ge0
\quad\text{for every admissible non-negative choice of the remaining coordinates}.
\label{eq:Sfacecondition}
\end{equation}
For reactor coordinates, non-negative inflow supplies the boundary and every consuming process must vanish there; for a Clarifier layer, the upwind advective and settling fluxes leave no negative outflow when its own solids concentration is zero. In particular, NOB growth includes $M(S_{NH4};K_{NH4,NOB})$ and precipitation includes $M(S_{ALK};K_{ALK,PRE})$; without those factors the model could consume ammonium or alkalinity at a zero boundary.

\section{Sampling, partitioning, and five-fold cross-validation}

For the seven free controls in the case study, the statistical input has $d_z=7+20=27$ coordinates and the complete quadratic has $n_\phi=1+27+27(28)/2=406$ columns. The interrupted 50,000-target design used development-stream state 100042. When generation was stopped, 18,211 attempts were complete and 16,714 were accepted. Ordering accepted candidates by original candidate index gives $n_D=13371$ development rows followed by $n_T=3343$ frozen holdout rows. Ten influent-only scenario rows retain state 314159. The holdout is therefore a deterministic pre-fit partition of the interrupted development stream, not the originally planned independent test stream.

For block $b\in\{D,T\}$, candidate $c$ receives a stable identifier containing the block, zero-based round, and zero-based within-round index. Round~0 candidates retain their original identifiers and inputs. After all candidates in a round have been adjudicated, let $d_b$ be the number of still-unfilled accepted slots. If $d_b>0$, the next supplemental round contains exactly $d_b$ candidates. It continues from that block's persisted final SplitMix64 state and draw count and consumes 27 successive midpoint-open coordinates per candidate, in row-major order,
\begin{equation}
u_{cj}=\frac{(U_{cj}\!\gg11)+1/2}{2^{53}},
\qquad j=1,\ldots,27,
\label{eq:Ssupplementaluniform}
\end{equation}
followed by the same affine maps to the declared bounds. For the frozen analysis no supplemental round is opened after the stop instruction. Thus the downstream data contain exactly $n_D+n_T=16714$ accepted mechanistic inputs; all 1,497 rejected completed attempts remain audit evidence and are excluded.

Every attempt persists its candidate identifier, input, solver status, and all available balance, non-negativity, domain, two-start, stability, and branch diagnostics. For every selected accepted row the record additionally stores its frozen partition and source-candidate provenance. The assembled mechanistic arrays and diagnostics used by fitting contain accepted rows only; a separate attempt ledger retains all completed failures. Per block these are published as \texttt{mechanistic\_accepted\_v3.npz}, \texttt{accepted\_inputs.npz}, \texttt{accepted\_diagnostics.csv}, \texttt{all\_attempts.csv}, and \texttt{accepted\_provenance.csv}. Original checkpoint bytes remain unchanged as historical evidence. Attempt acceptance rates use all 18,211 completed attempts as denominator, whereas fitting, cross-validation, and holdout metrics use $n_D=13371$ and $n_T=3343$ accepted rows, respectively.

Only candidate round~0 is a strength-one Latin hypercube. Because rejection is based on the mechanistic solver and physical acceptance contract, the completed accepted block is distributed conditionally on that contract, and its union of round~0 survivors and row-major supplemental draws is not a single global Latin hypercube. This conditioning can reduce coverage near difficult or switching regions. The study therefore reports candidate counts and reasons for rejection, round counts, coordinate coverage of the accepted set, and the complete source-candidate mapping; it does not describe the accepted rows as an unconditional space-filling sample of the whole box. ``Untouched test'' means only that accepted test responses are excluded from every fitting, scaling, ridge-selection, trust-calibration, and tuning operation.

The same article run is migrated rather than restarted. Migration verifies every existing round~0 checkpoint against its original contract, input bytes, and SHA-256 digest; preserves the prior contract and failure history in a migration ledger; assigns accepted indices and final slots without rewriting accepted mechanistic states; and then opens the supplemental stream. A migrated checkpoint that fails verification is not trusted or silently recomputed: it is reported as a run-integrity error. Subsequent resumption accepts a checkpoint only when its profile, dimensions, candidate input, provenance, and active contract hashes match exactly.

For exact reproducibility, one SplitMix64 word is generated modulo $2^{64}$ by
\begin{align}
z_0&\leftarrow z_0+\mathtt{9E3779B97F4A7C15},\notag\\
z_1&\leftarrow(z_0\oplus(z_0\!\gg30))\mathtt{BF58476D1CE4E5B9},\notag\\
z_2&\leftarrow(z_1\oplus(z_1\!\gg27))\mathtt{94D049BB133111EB},\notag\\
U&\leftarrow z_2\oplus(z_2\!\gg31).
\end{align}
In candidate round~0, each dimension consumes an unbiased Fisher--Yates permutation followed by its row jitters $[(U\!\gg11)+1/2]/2^{53}$. Dimensions are consumed in the order $(H,a_3,a_4,a_5,r_I,r_R,w,x_1,\ldots,x_{20})$, and affine maps convert unit coordinates to the declared bounds. Rejection sampling is used rather than modular reduction whenever a permutation range does not divide $2^{64}$. Supplemental candidates use Equation~\eqref{eq:Ssupplementaluniform} instead of permutations but retain the same dimension order and affine maps.

The feature calculation is restated here so that fitting can be reproduced without an implicit preprocessing step. For $n$ scalar observations $v_i$ in a fitting set $\mathcal I$, define the population mean and scale
\begin{equation}
\bar v_{\mathcal I}=\frac1n\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}v_i,
\qquad
\operatorname{sd}_{\mathcal I}(v)=
\left[\frac1n\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}(v_i-\bar v_{\mathcal I})^2\right]^{1/2}.
\label{eq:Ssd}
\end{equation}
Let $D_{\vartheta,{\rm fit}}$ and $D_{x,{\rm fit}}$ contain the coordinatewise scales from Equation~\eqref{eq:Ssd}. For each row,
\begin{equation}
z_i=\col[D_{\vartheta,{\rm fit}}^{-1}(\vartheta_i-\bar\vartheta),
D_{x,{\rm fit}}^{-1}(x_i-\bar x)],\qquad
r_i=\col[z_i,\vech(z_iz_i^\top)].
\end{equation}
With $D_{r,{\rm fit}}$ formed from the scales of the coordinates of $r_i$, the feature and standardized response are
\begin{equation}
\phi_i=\col[1,D_{r,{\rm fit}}^{-1}(r_i-\bar r)],
\qquad
\widetilde\chi_i=D_\chi^{-1}(\chi_i-\bar\chi).
\label{eq:Sstandardization}
\end{equation}
Here $D_\chi=\diag(\sigma_{\chi,1},\ldots,\sigma_{\chi,n_\chi})$ contains the response scales from Equation~\eqref{eq:Ssd}. Rows $\phi_i^\top$ and $\widetilde\chi_i^\top$ form $\Phi$ and $\widetilde Y$, respectively. All bars and diagonal scales refer only to the current fitting set.

Development rows are permuted once from state 271828 and divided consecutively into five folds of $n_D/5=800$. For each fold and each strictly positive ridge candidate
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R_{\rm ridge}=\{10^{-8},10^{-7},10^{-6},10^{-5},10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1},1,10,100\},
\end{equation}
all input, monomial, and response centers and population standard deviations are recomputed from the $4n_D/5=3200$ fitting rows. A coordinate is accepted only if
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{sd}_{\mathcal I}(v)>10^{-12}\max(1,\max_{i\in\mathcal I}|v_i|).
\end{equation}
The leading intercept is exempt. Failure for any required coordinate stops model development rather than silently dropping a response or monomial.

For fold $k$, raw validation score is
\begin{equation}
R_k(\gamma_{\rm R})=\left[\frac{1}{|\mathcal I_k^{\rm val}|n_\chi}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_k^{\rm val}}\left\|D_{\chi,-k}^{-1}\left(\chi_i-\chi_{{\rm raw},-k,\gamma_{\rm R}}(\vartheta_i,x_i)\right)\right\|_2^2\right]^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Let $\overline R(\gamma_{\rm R})=5^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^5R_k(\gamma_{\rm R})$ and
\begin{equation}
SE(\gamma_{\rm R})=
\left[\frac{1}{5(5-1)}\sum_{k=1}^5
\{R_k(\gamma_{\rm R})-\overline R(\gamma_{\rm R})\}^2\right]^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
If $\gamma_{\rm R,min}$ minimizes the mean, the selected value is the largest candidate satisfying
\begin{equation}
\overline R(\gamma_{\rm R})\le\overline R(\gamma_{\rm R,min})+SE(\gamma_{\rm R,min}).
\end{equation}
An exact tie uses the larger $\gamma_{\rm R}$. This is the one-standard-error rule \citep{Hastie2009}. Because the accepted rows are an acceptance-conditioned mixture of round~0 Latin-hypercube survivors and deterministic supplemental candidates rather than an iid sample, $SE$ is used only as a descriptive fold-stability rule, not as inferential sampling uncertainty. Projection errors and correction magnitudes are retained for every fold but do not select the parameter.

The final model is fitted on all $n_D$ development rows with the selected value. Ridge regression is solved by a column-pivoted QR factorization of the augmented system
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}\Phi\\\sqrt{n_D\gamma_{\rm R}}\,R_\phi\end{bmatrix}}_{\displaystyle\Phi_{\rm aug}}C^\top
\approx\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde Y\\0\end{bmatrix},\qquad R_\phi=\diag(0,1,\ldots,1),
\end{equation}
The positive penalty and the nonzero intercept column make the penalized normal matrix positive definite; full rank of the unregularized $\Phi$ is therefore not required. Let $C_{\rm QR}$ and $C_{\rm SVD}$ be the two coefficient solutions, use $\|C\|_{\max}:=\max_{ab}|C_{ab}|$ for a coefficient matrix, and let $\kappa_{\rm aug}=\operatorname{cond}_2(\Phi_{\rm aug})$. Development stops if $\kappa_{\rm aug}\epsilon_{\rm mach}>10^{-8}$ or if
\begin{equation}
\frac{\|C_{\rm QR}-C_{\rm SVD}\|_{\max}}
{1+\max(\|C_{\rm QR}\|_{\max},\|C_{\rm SVD}\|_{\max})}
>100\,\kappa_{\rm aug}\epsilon_{\rm mach}.
\label{eq:Scoefaudit}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm R}=\Phi^\top\Phi+n_D\gamma_{\rm R}R_\phi^\top R_\phi,\qquad
\operatorname{df}_{\rm R}=\operatorname{tr}(\Phi M_{\rm R}^{-1}\Phi^\top).
\label{eq:Sridgedf}
\end{equation}
The condition number, audit residual, and $\operatorname{df}_{\rm R}$ are reported; the five folds differ in size by at most one row.

\section{Physical-projection construction and acceptance}

For equality row $r$, let $\mathcal T_{ir}^{(b)}$ be the signed physical terms at development row $i$ before cancellation. For an inequality row, define $\mathcal T_{ir}^{(g)}$ analogously. Row scales are
\begin{align}
(D_b)_{rr}&=\max\left\{\operatorname{unit}_r^{(b)},\left[\frac1{n_D}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_{\rm dev}}\frac1{|\mathcal T_{ir}^{(b)}|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{ir}^{(b)}}t^2\right]^{1/2}\right\},\\
(D_g)_{rr}&=\max\left\{\operatorname{unit}_r^{(g)},\left[\frac1{n_D}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_{\rm dev}}\frac1{|\mathcal T_{ir}^{(g)}|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{ir}^{(g)}}t^2\right]^{1/2}\right\}.
\end{align}
Here $\operatorname{unit}_r^{(b)}$ and $\operatorname{unit}_r^{(g)}$ denote one unit in the corresponding row's physical units. These scales are not residual scales and remain positive even when mechanistic balances close exactly.

Write the right-hand side of the projection equalities as $\mathfrak b(\vartheta,x)$, consistently with the main article. The standardized projection matrices are
\begin{equation}
\mathsf E=D_b^{-1}\mathcal H D_\chi,\quad
e=D_b^{-1}(\mathfrak b-\mathcal H\chi_{\rm raw}),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathsf G_u=\begin{bmatrix}-I_{n_\chi}\\D_g^{-1}\mathcal GD_\chi\end{bmatrix},\qquad
g=\begin{bmatrix}D_\chi^{-1}\chi_{\rm raw}\\-D_g^{-1}\mathcal G\chi_{\rm raw}\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Thus the QP is $\min_u\|u\|_2^2/2$ subject to $\mathsf Eu=e$ and $\mathsf G_u u\le g$. The equality matrix must have numerical row rank $n_h$; in the case study $n_h=77$. Set $\tau_{\rm act}=10^{-7}$ and define the numerical active set
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_Q(u)=\{j:(\mathsf G_u u-g)_j\ge-\tau_{\rm act}\}.
\label{eq:SQPactive}
\end{equation}
After solving for $u$, set every inactive inequality multiplier to zero and recover the remaining multipliers from
\begin{equation}
\min_{\alpha,\,\beta\ge0}
\left\|u+\mathsf E^\top\alpha+
\mathsf G_{u,\mathcal A_Q}^\top\beta\right\|_2^2,
\qquad
\lambda^{=}=\alpha,\quad
\lambda_{\mathcal A_Q}^{\le}=\beta.
\label{eq:SQPmultiplier}
\end{equation}
The bounded problem in Equation~\eqref{eq:SQPmultiplier} is solved by the bounded-variable least-squares (BVLS) active-set algorithm. This choice is deterministic for the fixed matrix ordering and directly enforces $\beta\ge0$. If the multiplier representation is not unique, the reported pair is the deterministic BVLS representation; no minimum-multiplier-norm claim is made. Thus the dual audit is reconstructed from the reported primal point and matrices rather than inherited from a solver-specific sign convention. The reconstruction is accepted only after the stationarity vector is recomputed from the reported primal and reconstructed multipliers. A QP result is accepted only if all arrays are finite and
\begin{equation}
r_E,r_G,r_{stat},r_{dual},r_{comp}\le10^{-8},\qquad r_+\le10^{-10},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
r_E&=\|\mathsf Eu-e\|_\infty,&r_G&=\|\pos{D_g^{-1}\mathcal G\widehat\chi}\|_\infty,\\
r_+&=\|\pos{-D_\chi^{-1}\widehat\chi}\|_\infty,&
r_{dual}&=\|\pos{-\lambda^{\le}}\|_\infty,\\
r_{stat}&=\max_k\frac{|u_k+(\mathsf E^\top\lambda^{=})_k+
(\mathsf G_u^\top\lambda^{\le})_k|}
{1+|u_k|+|(\mathsf E^\top\lambda^{=})_k|+
|(\mathsf G_u^\top\lambda^{\le})_k|},\\
r_{comp}&=\|\lambda^{\le}\odot(g-\mathsf G_u u)\|_\infty.
\end{align}
Every final candidate is resolved from a cold start and these residuals are recomputed independently. If the first cold OSQP solution fails that audit, two further independently initialized cold solves are attempted without changing the QP: first with $\rho=0.01$ and then with $\rho=10$, both with adaptive-$\rho$ disabled and at most 200,000 iterations. Each candidate undergoes the complete audit above; the first accepted candidate is used. If none passes, the candidate with the smallest ordered residual score is returned for diagnosis, the attempt count and fallback status are recorded, and the projection remains scientifically failed. This retry changes neither the objective nor its feasible set and is not regularization.

For completeness, the engineering rows are evaluated without unsafe trial-point division. Let
\begin{equation}
M_X=\sum_{i=1}^{N}V_i t_X^\top c_i+\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}V_{{\rm cl},\ell}s_\ell,
\qquad B_X=t_X^\top g_E+\frac{w}{q_U}t_X^\top g_U.
\end{equation}
Because $Q_0B_X$ is the external solids loss rate, the SRT bounds $T_{\rm SRT}^L$ and $T_{\rm SRT}^U$ are imposed as
\begin{equation}
T_{\rm SRT}^LQ_0B_X-M_X\le0,\qquad
M_X-T_{\rm SRT}^UQ_0B_X\le0,\qquad B_X\ge B_X^{\min}>0.
\end{equation}
The case uses $B_X^{\min}=1$ g TSS m$^{-3}$, so the mass-rate guard in the main article is $\epsilon_X=Q_0B_X^{\min}$. The remaining rows are
\begin{equation}
\frac{10^{-3}Q_0q_Ct_X^\top c_N}{A_{\rm cl}}\le100,\qquad
\frac{t_X^\top g_U}{q_U}\le15{,}000,\qquad t_X^\top c_N\ge1,
\end{equation}
in their units stated in the main article. SOR is only reported because $Q_0q_E/A_{\rm cl}\le20$ is redundant throughout the case box.

The upper trust diagnostics are now explicit. The correction diagnostic is
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{\rm corr}^2=\frac{u^\top u}{n_\chi}.
\end{equation}
Using the final-fit matrix $M_{\rm R}$ from Equation~\eqref{eq:Sridgedf}, regularized feature leverage at a proposed input is
\begin{equation}
\ell_{\rm R}(\vartheta,x)=\phi(\vartheta,x)^\top M_{\rm R}^{-1}\phi(\vartheta,x).
\end{equation}
The convex projection intentionally permits particulate components to have different recoveries, whereas the reduced mechanistic Clarifier assumes one common particulate composition. A denominator-free diagnostic of that relaxation is
\begin{equation}
r_{\rm split}=D_{\rm split}^{-1}
\left[(t_X^\top c_N)E_Xg_U-(t_X^\top g_U)E_Xc_N\right],
\qquad \kappa_{\rm split}^2=\frac{r_{\rm split}^\top r_{\rm split}}{F_X},
\end{equation}
For particulate coordinate $j$, write $p_{ij}^{(1)}=(t_X^\top c_{N,i})(E_Xg_{U,i})_j$ and $p_{ij}^{(2)}=(t_X^\top g_{U,i})(E_Xc_{N,i})_j$. Its positive scale is defined without using the nearly cancelling residual:
\begin{equation}
(D_{\rm split})_{jj}=\max\left\{\operatorname{unit}_j^{\rm split},
\left[\frac1{2n_D}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_{\rm dev}}
\{(p_{ij}^{(1)})^2+(p_{ij}^{(2)})^2\}\right]^{1/2}\right\},
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{unit}_j^{\rm split}$ is the product of one unit of TSS concentration and one unit of particulate-component flow concentration. Conservation alone is not a kinetic test, so the projected reactors are also substituted into the smooth rate equations. With $\rho_{\epsilon}$ defined below, let
\begin{equation}
f_{{\rm rxn},\epsilon}=\col\!\left(
\left\{d_i(c_{i-1}-c_i)+\nu^\top\rho_\epsilon(c_i)
+\omega_i e_{S_O}+\xi_i\right\}_{i=1}^{N}\right),
\qquad
\kappa_{\rm rxn}^2=\frac{\|D_{\rm rxn}^{-1}f_{{\rm rxn},\epsilon}\|_2^2}{NF}.
\end{equation}
If $f_{{\rm cl},\epsilon}(s;c_N,\vartheta)\in\R^L$ denotes the smooth layer residual defined below, define
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{\rm flux}^2=\frac{1}{L}
\left\|D_{\rm cl}^{-1}f_{{\rm cl},\epsilon}(s;c_N,\vartheta)\right\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
For either residual family, let $\mathcal T_{ia}$ be its separately evaluated, volume-scaled physical terms at mechanistic development target $i$ (dilution, reaction, aeration, and addition for a reactor row; incoming flux, outgoing flux, and feed for a Clarifier row). At the final smoothing pair $(\epsilon,\tau_X)=(10^{-8},1\ \mathrm{g\ TSS\ m}^{-3})$, its diagonal scale is
\begin{equation}
(D_f)_{aa}=\max\left\{\operatorname{unit}_a,
\left[\frac1{n_D}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_{\rm dev}}\frac1{|\mathcal T_{ia}|}
\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{ia}}t^2\right]^{1/2}\right\},
\qquad D_f\in\{D_{\rm rxn},D_{\rm cl}\},
\label{eq:Sbalancescales}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{unit}_a$ is one unit of concentration per day for that row. Equation~\eqref{eq:Sbalancescales} completely specifies the residual scales used by the trust constraints, optimizer audits, and exact-reference diagnostics.

The optimization imposes the differentiable squared forms $\kappa_{\rm corr}^2\le\bar\kappa_{\rm corr}^2$, $\ell_{\rm R}\le\bar\ell$, $\kappa_{\rm split}^2\le\bar\kappa_{\rm split}^2$, $\kappa_{\rm rxn}^2\le\bar\kappa_{\rm rxn}^2$, and $\kappa_{\rm flux}^2\le\bar\kappa_{\rm flux}^2$. The four $\bar\kappa$ limits are nearest-rank 95th percentiles computed from the corresponding out-of-fold projected development predictions, with the reactor and Clarifier quantities evaluated at the final smoothing pair; $\bar\ell$ is the maximum development-row leverage under the final fit. The scientific model-development gate fails if $\bar\kappa_{\rm corr}>0.50$: that gate prevents a run from being treated as article-eligible when the projection makes an RMS repair larger than one-half development response standard deviation. These screens constrain the upper problem; they do not alter the lower projection. For the model-function execution, every finite out-of-fold projection and its full audit are retained; a failed audit marks the gate and all dependent thresholds as scientifically failed but does not by itself halt subsequent workflow exercises. A non-finite or incomplete projection, undefined scale, or other condition that makes a downstream calculation mathematically undefined remains fatal.

Finite solver tolerance means a generated target need not lie exactly in the ideal projection polyhedron. Let $\mathcal C_i$ denote that polyhedron for row $i$, let $P_{\mathcal C_i}$ denote projection in the norm $\|v\|_{D_\chi^{-1}}:=\|D_\chi^{-1}v\|_2$, and define
\begin{equation}
\delta_{{\rm feas},i}=\|D_\chi^{-1}[P_{\mathcal C_i}(\chi_i)-\chi_i]\|_2
\end{equation}
for every target. The valid bound
\begin{equation}
\|D_\chi^{-1}[\widehat\chi_i-\chi_i]\|_2
\le \|D_\chi^{-1}[\chi_{{\rm raw},i}-\chi_i]\|_2+\delta_{{\rm feas},i}
\end{equation}
is checked for every numerical target. The stronger squared projection inequality is used only if $\delta_{{\rm feas},i}=0$ in exact arithmetic, never merely because the value is small. The holdout admission gate also requires all $n_T=3343$ QPs to pass the primal--dual contract and complete-state raw nRMSE to be below one. Here complete-state means the row with block ``complete response'' and coordinate ``ALL'', not the first coordinate-level metric. The nRMSE criterion means that the root-mean-square error over all holdout rows and response coordinates, after coordinatewise development scaling, is less than one development response standard deviation on average. It is a predeclared adequacy gate rather than a comparison with a separately fitted baseline. Failure is recorded without changing $\gamma_{\rm R}$ or refitting and makes the run ineligible as scientific article evidence. Under the explicitly authorized model-function policy, however, it does not prevent the remaining optimization, replay, timing, or report paths from being exercised. This is continuation after failure, not a gate bypass: every downstream artifact carries the failed-gate status, and the final scientific-validation flag remains false.

The execution layer distinguishes scientific rejection from inability to compute. A finite, dimensionally complete result that fails a declared residual or adequacy threshold is a scientific diagnostic and is retained while the model-function workflow proceeds. Missing or non-finite arrays, an undefined feature or response scale, a rank or factorization failure that prevents construction of the stated problem, and a contract, hash, or checkpoint inconsistency are hard computational or integrity failures and stop execution. A failed mechanistic generation is the separately declared exception: it is retained and deterministically replaced before fitting, as specified above. Assessment rows are never discarded or replaced after model fitting.

\section{Surrogate-optimization solution protocol}

For primal-feasible $u$ and dual-feasible $\lambda^{\le}$, the non-negative primal--dual gap used in the independent QP audit is
\begin{equation}
\Gamma=\tfrac12\|u+\mathsf E^\top\lambda^{=}+\mathsf G_u^\top\lambda^{\le}\|_2^2
+(\lambda^{\le})^\top(g-\mathsf G_u u).
\end{equation}
At zero gap, both stationarity and every complementary product vanish by convex strong duality \citep{Boyd2004}. An earlier production plan embedded the controls, displacement, and both multiplier vectors in a 450-variable single-level NLP and tightened $\overline\Gamma=\Gamma/(n_\chi+n_q)$ through
\begin{equation}
\tau\in\{10^{-2},10^{-4},10^{-6},3\times10^{-7},10^{-7},3\times10^{-8},10^{-8}\}.
\end{equation}
That embedded-KKT continuation and its nine-start center-plus-Latin-hypercube search are retired for production. No surrogate IPOPT solve, gap stage, or dual warm start is executed. The production calculation instead starts once per case at
\begin{equation}
z^{(0)}=\tfrac12\mathbf 1_7,\qquad
\vartheta^{(0)}=\vartheta^L+(\vartheta^U-\vartheta^L)\odot z^{(0)},
\end{equation}
and applies the exact-QP active-set method directly in $z\in[0,1]^7$. A newly initialized OSQP instance resolves the original projection QP to the acceptance tolerances at every distinct trial control; cached values may avoid repeated evaluation of an identical trial but no QP warm start crosses distinct controls. The primary upper search uses analytical total gradients from the active lower-QP KKT system. Only this one center-initiated search is attempted. The selected result is subsequently assigned either the strong first-order KKT tier, the finite-resolution feasible-poll tier, or an unresolved classification under the independent certification below; none supplies a global certificate.

Every upper inequality is represented as $c_{{\rm up},r}=\widetilde c_{{\rm up},r}/s_r\le0$ with a fixed positive $s_r$. The two cross-multiplied SRT rows use the fixed inventory scale $30Q_0X_U^{\max}$; external-loss, SLR, underflow-TSS, feed-TSS, and any SOR rows use their respective positive declared limits. A trust diagnostic $v_r\le L_r$ uses $(v_r-L_r)/L_r$ when $L_r>0$ and $(v_r-L_r)/1$ for an exact-zero limit. Control-bound rows use their positive coordinate spans. These scales are frozen before optimization, never depend on the current iterate, preserve the original feasible set, and place the upper rows near order one. An unavailable active-set derivative invokes the fallback below. A failed initial projection, fallback, final cold replay, or endpoint audit is stored with the case rather than terminating attempts for the remaining nominal or robustness cases.

On a candidate lower active set, the equality rows and active inequalities must have full numerical row rank, the active KKT matrix must satisfy $\kappa_2\epsilon_{\rm mach}\le10^{-8}$, every active multiplier must exceed $10^{-8}$, and normalized control perturbations of $10^{-6}$ in every domain-feasible direction must preserve the active indices and multiplier signs. The exact total derivatives of projected state, objective, and upper rows are then obtained by differentiating that equality-plus-active-inequality KKT system; there is no finite-difference fallback. An active-set change triggers a new cold QP and derivative reconstruction. Let $D_{\vartheta,{\rm span}}=\diag(\vartheta^U-\vartheta^L)$ contain the positive case-study decision spans and let $\mathcal A_U=\{j:c_{{\rm up},j}\ge-\tau_{\rm act}\}$. Inactive multipliers are set to zero; the unrestricted $\eta_{\rm up}$ and active non-negative $\zeta_{{\rm up},\mathcal A_U}$ minimize
\begin{equation}
\left\|D_{\vartheta,{\rm span}}\left[
\nabla_\vartheta J+J_{a_{\rm up}}^\top\eta_{\rm up}
+J_{c_{\rm up},\mathcal A_U}^\top\zeta_{{\rm up},\mathcal A_U}
\right]\right\|_2^2,
\label{eq:Suppermultiplier}
\end{equation}
with the minimum-norm multiplier pair selected among ties. Here $J_a$ denotes the Jacobian of constraint vector $a$, not the scalar engineering objective $J$. Every derivative in Equation~\eqref{eq:Suppermultiplier}, and subsequently in $r_{\rm upper}$, is a total derivative with respect to $\vartheta$ through the independently resolved projection QP; the projected response is not held fixed. The reduced upper Lagrangian is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_U=J(\vartheta,\widehat\chi(\vartheta,x))
+\eta_{\rm up}^\top a_{\rm up}(\vartheta)
+\zeta_{\rm up}^\top c_{\rm up}(\vartheta).
\end{equation}
For lower-QP sensitivity, set
\begin{equation}
B_Q=\begin{bmatrix}\mathsf E\\\mathsf G_{u,\mathcal A_Q}\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
K_Q=\begin{bmatrix}I_{n_\chi}&B_Q^\top\\B_Q&0\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:SQPKKTmatrix}
\end{equation}
With the active set held fixed, collect the primal and active multipliers in $v_Q=\col(u,\lambda^{=},\lambda_{\mathcal A_Q}^{\le})$ and the corresponding right-hand side in $b_Q=\col(0_{n_\chi},e,g_{\mathcal A_Q})$. The active KKT equations are therefore
\begin{equation}
K_Qv_Q=b_Q,
\qquad
K_Q\frac{\partial v_Q}{\partial\vartheta_k}
=\frac{\partial b_Q}{\partial\vartheta_k}
-\frac{\partial K_Q}{\partial\vartheta_k}v_Q,
\quad k=1,\ldots,d_\vartheta.
\label{eq:SQPKKTsensitivity}
\end{equation}
The derivative $dv_Q/d\vartheta$ is accepted only if $\operatorname{rank}_{\rm num}(B_Q)=n_h+|\mathcal A_Q|$, $\kappa_2(K_Q)\epsilon_{\rm mach}\le10^{-8}$, every active multiplier exceeds $\tau_{\rm mult}=10^{-8}$, and domain-aware normalized-control perturbations of magnitude $10^{-6}$ preserve the active set and these multiplier signs. Under those conditions Equation~\eqref{eq:SQPKKTsensitivity} is solved once per control coordinate. The gradient below is the total derivative through the resolved projection QP:
\begin{equation}
r_{\rm upper}=\|D_{\vartheta,{\rm span}}\nabla_\vartheta
\mathcal L_U\|_\infty.
\end{equation}
A failure of any analytical active-set derivative condition does not substitute a finite-difference derivative. Instead, deterministic value-only COBYQA restarts from the same normalized center and evaluates the identical objective and normalized upper constraints. Its box bounds are unrelaxable, its initial trust-region radius is 0.25, its final radius and feasibility tolerance are $10^{-6}$, and both its iteration and distinct-evaluation budgets are 250. Every distinct fallback trial cold-solves and audits the unchanged projection QP; only exact duplicate controls share a cached value, and no QP warm start crosses controls. A failed QP evaluation is recorded and cannot enter the feasible candidate pool.

After COBYQA terminates, the feasible visited point with the smallest independently evaluated objective is retained, using the relative $10^{-10}$ objective and lexicographic-control tie rules. That point is cold-replayed, and the analytical lower active-set and upper-KKT audits are attempted at the replayed endpoint. COBYQA values alone cannot establish stationarity. Its evaluation-budget status, solver message, failed QP evaluations, replay residuals, and endpoint audits are persisted, but the final convergence qualification is assigned only by the independent certification below.

A point receives the strong first-order KKT certificate only if $r_{\rm upper}\le10^{-6}$ and
\begin{equation}
\max\{\|a_{\rm up}\|_\infty,\|\pos{c_{\rm up}}\|_\infty\},\quad
\|\pos{-\zeta_{\rm up}}\|_\infty,\quad
\|\zeta_{\rm up}\odot c_{\rm up}\|_\infty
\end{equation}
are each no greater than $10^{-6}$. An active-set change triggers a fresh QP and derivative reconstruction. Interior controls are additionally perturbed in both directions. If any rank, conditioning, strict-complementarity, or active-set test fails, the strong certificate is unavailable.

When the strong audit is unavailable, deterministic exact-QP feasible-poll protocol v3 is attempted. At the coarse normalized radius $\rho_1=10^{-3}$ the poll set is the 14 signed coordinate directions $\{\pm e_j\}_{j=1}^7$. At the fine radius $\rho_2=10^{-4}$ the declared 106-direction set comprises those axes, the 84 signed pairwise diagonals
\begin{equation}
\left\{\frac{s_i e_i+s_j e_j}{\sqrt{2}}:
1\le i<j\le7,\quad s_i,s_j\in\{-1,1\}\right\},
\end{equation}
and the eight unit vertices of the regular seven-dimensional Helmert simplex. Trial controls are clipped to $[0,1]^7$, duplicate or zero displacements are omitted, and every remaining trial is independently exact-QP evaluated. A trial is feasible only when every normalized upper violation is at most $10^{-6}$. Relative to the current center, sufficient decrease means
\begin{equation}
J(z_{\rm trial})<J(z_{\rm center})-\max\{10^{-8},10^{-8}|J(z_{\rm center})|\}.
\label{eq:Ssufficientdecrease}
\end{equation}
The best sufficiently improving feasible trial is accepted, with lexicographic control order breaking objective ties. Search then tests the same exact-QP-feasible coupled ray at multipliers $2,4,\ldots$, stopping at the first insufficient decrease, infeasible point, box boundary, QP failure, or after 16 probes. Each accepted acceleration point must improve sufficiently relative to the current incumbent. These ray trials are search steps only: the radius is always polled again from the final accepted point, and no ray trial is used as no-descent evidence. A level passes only after a complete sweep has no sufficient feasible decrease and contains at least one feasible nonzero displacement. Any required QP failure makes that sweep incomplete. The numerical rank of the normalized feasible displacements is computed at tolerance $10^{-10}$ and retained as a coverage diagnostic, but is not a pass/fail condition: at a constrained endpoint the active feasible cone need not linearly span $\mathbb R^7$. If the fine poll accepts any move, every earlier coarse result is stale and the sequence restarts at $\rho_1$ from the new center. Certification requires a final no-descent coarse poll followed by a final no-descent fine poll at the same endpoint, within a shared safety budget of 10,000 distinct exact-QP evaluations.

The final poll point is cold-replayed and the strong KKT audit is retried. If it passes, the endpoint receives the strong first-order certificate. Otherwise, passing both complete poll levels gives the classification ``finite-resolution feasible-poll converged, stationarity unresolved.'' This is evidence of no detected feasible decrease only for the finite declared directions, tolerances, and radii; it is not a KKT, Clarke-stationarity, mathematical local-optimality, or global-optimality certificate. Exhausting the evaluation budget, failing a required QP, finding no feasible nonzero displacement at a completed level, or failing the final replay yields an inconclusive or budget-limited poll and retains the feasible incumbent without a convergence certificate. A low feasible-direction rank is reported but does not itself cause failure. A relative $10^{-10}$ search-objective tie uses lexicographically smaller normalized controls. Even the strong first-order audit is not proof of local or global minimality; nonconvexity precludes a global certificate.

Protocol v1 used only 22 fine directions, a 120-evaluation budget, and a full-rank acceptance requirement. Its preliminary outputs left all eleven cases unresolved: three were budget-limited and eight were rank-inconclusive. Protocol v2 corrected those rules, but its fixed-step fine search in robustness case 05 accepted 36 moves and exhausted 2,500 distinct exact-QP evaluations before completing the next sweep. This was a search-efficiency limitation, not an exact-QP or upper-feasibility failure. The v1 and v2 artifacts are preserved with superseded, excluded-from-analysis status and are not relabeled. Only the uniformly rerun v3 certification outcomes enter the article analysis. The v3 rerun occupies a new self-contained result directory; a hash-complete copy manifest identifies every inherited dataset, fitted, assessment, and primary-search artifact and its source run.

\section{Smooth mechanistic NLP and exact-reference adjudication}

The direct NLP contains the seven case controls, $n_y=110$ reduced mechanistic states, and one Clarifier-feed auxiliary, for 118 primal variables. The auxiliary is not free: it obeys
\begin{equation}
\widetilde X_F=t_X^\top c_N,\qquad
\widetilde X_F\ge X_F^{\min}=1\ \mathrm{g\ TSS\ m}^{-3}.
\label{eq:Sfeedaux}
\end{equation}
At a feasible point it is exactly the final-reactor TSS. Its lower bound merely keeps the common-composition quotient defined while an NLP iterate is infeasible. Let $\mu_y$ contain development medians and define
\begin{equation}
D_y=\diag\!\left(\max\{\operatorname{unit}(y_j),\operatorname{MAD}_{\rm dev}(y_{\cdot j})\}\right)_{j=1}^{n_y},
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{unit}(y_j)$ has the meaning defined above and the unscaled median absolute deviation is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{MAD}_{\rm dev}(y_{\cdot j})
=\operatorname{median}_{i\in\mathcal I_{\rm dev}}
\left|y_{ij}-\operatorname{median}_{k\in\mathcal I_{\rm dev}}y_{kj}\right|.
\label{eq:Smad}
\end{equation}
No normal-consistency factor is applied. Define also
\begin{equation}
\sigma_F=\max\{1\ \mathrm{g\ TSS\ m}^{-3},
\operatorname{RMS}_{\rm dev}(X_F)\},\qquad
D_{\rm bal}=\operatorname{blkdiag}(D_{\rm rxn},D_{\rm cl}).
\label{eq:Sdirectscales}
\end{equation}
Here
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{RMS}_{\rm dev}(X_F)=
\left(\frac1{n_D}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_{\rm dev}}X_{F,i}^2\right)^{1/2}.
\label{eq:Sfeedrms}
\end{equation}
The internal state variable is $D_y^{-1}(y-\mu_y)$. Reactor and Clarifier residuals use $D_{\rm rxn}$ and $D_{\rm cl}$ defined above. The direct NLP also imposes the same layer envelope as the surrogate route, so neither optimizer can select a profile that target generation would reject.

Only operations that are nonsmooth or have a possible zero denominator are regularized. For a variable with scale $S>0$, define
\begin{align}
\operatorname{smax}_{\epsilon,S}(a,b)&=\tfrac12[a+b+\sqrt{(a-b)^2+(\epsilon S)^2}],\\
\operatorname{smin}_{\epsilon,S}(a,b)&=\tfrac12[a+b-\sqrt{(a-b)^2+(\epsilon S)^2}],\\
p_{\epsilon,S}(v)&=
\begin{cases}
[v+\operatorname{hypot}(v,\epsilon S)]/2,&v\ge0,\\
(\epsilon S)^2/[2\{\operatorname{hypot}(v,\epsilon S)-v\}],&v<0,
\end{cases}\\
\operatorname{sdiv}_{\epsilon,S}(a,b)&=\frac{ab}{b^2+(\epsilon S)^2}.
\end{align}
The two branches defining $p_{\epsilon,S}$ are algebraically identical to $(v+\sqrt{v^2+(\epsilon S)^2})/2$, but the negative branch avoids catastrophic cancellation and a spurious numerical zero. The last expression approximates $a/b$ and is used only when $b$ can be zero. For every reactor-state denominator $b$, define the stage-pooled scale
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{RMS}_{\rm dev}(b)=
\left[\frac1{n_DN}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_{\rm dev}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}
b(c_{ik})^2\right]^{1/2},\qquad
\sigma_b=\max\{\operatorname{unit}(b),\operatorname{RMS}_{\rm dev}(b)\},
\label{eq:Sdenomrms}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{unit}(b)$ is one unit of that denominator. The complete smooth kinetic map is defined by
\begin{align}
\alpha_{2,\epsilon}&=\operatorname{sdiv}_{\epsilon,\sigma_{NOx}}(S_{NO2},S_{NOx}),&
\alpha_{3,\epsilon}&=\operatorname{sdiv}_{\epsilon,\sigma_{NOx}}(S_{NO3},S_{NOx}),\\
\alpha_{F,\epsilon}&=\operatorname{sdiv}_{\epsilon,\sigma_{FA}}(S_F,S_F+S_A),&
\alpha_{A,\epsilon}&=\operatorname{sdiv}_{\epsilon,\sigma_{FA}}(S_A,S_F+S_A),\\
\Theta_{X,\epsilon}&=\operatorname{sdiv}_{\epsilon,\sigma_{hyd}}
(X_SX_H,K_XX_H+X_S),\\
\Psi_{PP,\epsilon}&=\operatorname{sdiv}_{\epsilon,\sigma_{PP}}
(X_{PP}X_{PAO},K_{PP}X_{PAO}+X_{PP}),\\
\Psi_{PHA,\epsilon}&=\operatorname{sdiv}_{\epsilon,\sigma_{PHA}}
(X_{PHA}X_{PAO},K_{PHA}X_{PAO}+X_{PHA}).
\label{eq:Ssmoothkineticshares}
\end{align}
Here $\sigma_{NOx}$, $\sigma_{FA}$, $\sigma_{hyd}$, $\sigma_{PP}$, and $\sigma_{PHA}$ are the Equation~\eqref{eq:Sdenomrms} scales of the respective second arguments. To smooth the storage-capacity switch without first dividing by $X_{PAO}$, define
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{PP,\epsilon}=p_{\epsilon,\sigma_{cap}}(K_{\max}X_{PAO}-X_{PP}),
\qquad
c_{PP,\epsilon}=\frac{\Delta_{PP,\epsilon}}
{K_{IPP}X_{PAO}+\Delta_{PP,\epsilon}},
\label{eq:Ssmoothcapacity}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{cap}=\max\{1\ \mathrm{g\ P\ m}^{-3},
\operatorname{RMS}_{\rm dev}(K_{\max}X_{PAO}-X_{PP})\},
\end{equation}
using the same $n_DN$ stage pooling as Equation~\eqref{eq:Sdenomrms}. The vector $\rho_\epsilon(c_i)$ is obtained from the 28 displayed rate laws by replacing $(\alpha_2,\alpha_3,\alpha_F,\alpha_A)$ by their smooth versions, every product $\theta_XX_H$ by $\Theta_{X,\epsilon}$, every product $\psi_{PP}X_{PAO}$ by $\Psi_{PP,\epsilon}$, every product $\psi_{PHA}X_{PAO}$ by $\Psi_{PHA,\epsilon}$, and $c_{PP}$ by $c_{PP,\epsilon}$. All Monod factors with strictly positive half-saturation denominators remain exact. This cancellation-safe construction makes every zero-denominator substitution explicit, including $X_{PAO}=0$.

Quantities with positive hydraulic guards use their ordinary quotient: in particular $c_E=g_E/q_E$, $c_U=g_U/q_U$, and the direct-NLP common particulate fraction $E_Xc_N/\widetilde X_F$ use $q_E>0$, $q_U>0$, and Equation~\eqref{eq:Sfeedaux}. This avoids an undefined trial-point quotient while preserving $t_X^\top c_{{\rm cl},\ell}=s_\ell$ exactly at every feasible point. In every smooth Clarifier expression below, $X_F$ is therefore replaced by $\widetilde X_F$ when the expression is evaluated inside the direct NLP.

For a layer-indexed development quantity $a_{i\ell}$, define
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{RMS}_{i,\ell}(a)=
\left[\frac1{n_DL}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_{\rm dev}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}a_{i\ell}^2\right]^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
The smoothing scales are then fixed from the development block:
\begin{equation}
S_X=\max\{1,\operatorname{RMS}_{i,\ell}|s_{i\ell}-f_{ns}X_{F,i}|\},\quad
S_v=\max\{1,v_0^{\max}\},\quad
S_J=\max\{1,\operatorname{RMS}_{i,\ell}j_s(s_{i\ell};X_{F,i})\}.
\label{eq:Ssmoothscales}
\end{equation}
Their units are, respectively, g TSS m$^{-3}$, m d$^{-1}$, and g TSS m$^{-2}$ d$^{-1}$. The smooth settling velocity is constructed in three visible steps:
\begin{align}
\Delta_\epsilon(s)&=p_{\epsilon,S_X}(s-f_{ns}\widetilde X_F),\\
v_{\rm raw,\epsilon}(s)&=v_0\{e^{-r_h\Delta_\epsilon(s)}-e^{-r_p\Delta_\epsilon(s)}\},\\
v_{s,\epsilon}(s)&=\operatorname{smax}_{\epsilon,S_v}
\{0,\operatorname{smin}_{\epsilon,S_v}(v_0^{\max},v_{\rm raw,\epsilon}(s))\},
\end{align}
and $j_{s,\epsilon}(s)=s v_{s,\epsilon}(s)\ge0$. This construction prevents exponential overflow and negative settling velocity at trial points.

The discontinuous receiver threshold uses a $C^2$ transition of half-width $\tau_X=1$ g TSS m$^{-3}$:
\begin{equation}
w_X(s)=\begin{cases}
0,&s\le X_t-\tau_X,\\
6\upsilon_X^5-15\upsilon_X^4+10\upsilon_X^3,&X_t-\tau_X<s<X_t+\tau_X,\\
1,&s\ge X_t+\tau_X,
\end{cases}\quad
\upsilon_X=\frac{s-X_t+\tau_X}{2\tau_X}.
\end{equation}
The square-root smooth minimum can itself be slightly negative when one non-negative argument is zero. The receiver limiter therefore applies a final smooth positive part:
\begin{equation}
j_{{\rm lim},\epsilon}(s_u,s_d)=[1-w_X(s_d)]j_{s,\epsilon}(s_u)+w_X(s_d)
p_{\epsilon,S_J}\!\left(\operatorname{smin}_{\epsilon,S_J}
[j_{s,\epsilon}(s_u),j_{s,\epsilon}(s_d)]\right).
\end{equation}
It replaces $j^{\rm set}$ in the otherwise unchanged advective and layer balances. The complete smooth residual is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
f_{\rm sm}=\col\!\Big(&
\{d_i(c_{i-1}-c_i)+\nu^\top\rho_{\epsilon}(c_i)
+\omega_i e_{S_O}+\xi_i\}_{i=1}^{N},\\
&\{V_{{\rm cl},\ell}^{-1}[A_{\rm cl}(\mathcal J_{\ell-1/2,\epsilon}-
\mathcal J_{\ell+1/2,\epsilon})
+\mathbf1_{\{\ell=\ell_F\}}Q_0q_C\widetilde X_F]\}_{\ell=1}^{L}\Big).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The final case settings are $\epsilon=10^{-8}$ and $\tau_X=1$ g TSS m$^{-3}$, but they are reached by the paired continuation
\begin{equation}
(\epsilon,\tau_X)=(10^{-6},10),\ (10^{-7},3),\ (10^{-8},1).
\end{equation}
Because $w_X(X_t)=1/2$ whereas the nonsmooth rule selects one branch, every comparison reports the active receiver branch, the kinetic storage-capacity positive-part branch, and the settling-velocity floor, cap, and receiver-flux minimum branches. A point inside a final smoothing band is marked branch-boundary ambiguous. That mark qualifies differentiability and smooth-model interpretation, but it does not reject an otherwise valid exact nonsmooth replay. A categorical disagreement between two required exact replays remains a root-agreement failure.

The primary direct NLP uses the same center control start $z^{(0)}=\mathbf1_7/2$ as the surrogate route. Define a generic positive initialization map from a non-negative base state $y_{\rm base}$:
\begin{equation}
y_j^{(0)}=\max\{y_{{\rm base},j},10^{-8}\operatorname{unit}(y_j)\},\qquad
\widetilde X_F^{(0)}=\max\{X_F^{\min},t_X^\top c_N^{(0)}\}.
\label{eq:Sdirectstart}
\end{equation}
For optimization, $y_{\rm base}$ is the target at the development row $i^\star$ minimizing
\begin{equation}
\left\|D_{\vartheta,{\rm fit}}^{-1}(\vartheta^{(0)}-\vartheta_i)\right\|_2^2+
\left\|D_{x,{\rm fit}}^{-1}(x-x_i)\right\|_2^2,
\qquad i\in\mathcal I_{\rm dev},
\label{eq:Sneareststate}
\end{equation}
where the scales come from the final $n_D$-row development fit and an exact tie uses the smaller development-row index. The floor affects only initialization, not the equations. The paired three-stage smoothing continuation is retained for the direct-mechanistic solve; it is distinct from, and unaffected by the retirement of, the surrogate gap continuation. Each direct stage uses IPOPT with at most 2500 iterations, adaptive barrier updates, and zero bound relaxation. Its algorithmic objective/constraint Jacobian and Lagrangian Hessian are audited in dimensionless internal coordinates against domain-aware second-order finite differences at steps $10^{-6}$ and $5\times10^{-7}$, with a relative column-discrepancy limit of $10^{-5}$.

The center start is always the primary direct attempt. If it supplies a selected candidate, no additional direct start is permitted. If it fails to do so, at most one recovery may be attempted, and only when the same case has a surrogate candidate certified either by the strong KKT tier or by the complete two-scale feasible-poll tier. The recovery control start is exactly that certified surrogate endpoint; its state initialization follows Equations~\eqref{eq:Sdirectstart}--\eqref{eq:Sneareststate} at the recovery control. It uses the unchanged three-stage continuation, limits, and endpoint audits. The record identifies whether the direct result came from the independent primary center start or the surrogate-dependent recovery. An uncertified or absent surrogate candidate authorizes no recovery, and a failed recovery leaves the direct route unpaired while later cases and audits continue.

The direct route has its own independently recomputed KKT contract. Let $p_M=\col(\vartheta,y,\widetilde X_F)$ and
\begin{equation}
D_M=\operatorname{blkdiag}(D_{\vartheta,{\rm span}},D_y,\sigma_F),\qquad
a_M(p_M)=\col\!\left(D_{\rm bal}^{-1}f_{\rm sm},
\frac{\widetilde X_F-t_X^\top c_N}{\sigma_F},a_{\mathcal V}(\vartheta)\right).
\label{eq:Sdirecteq}
\end{equation}
Here $a_{\mathcal V}$ contains any scaled stage-allocation equality in the operating set $\mathcal V$ and is empty in the present case. Let $D_{\rm env}$ and $D_{\rm eng,red}$ contain the corresponding fixed positive envelope and engineering row scales under the normalization rule above, and let $c_{\mathcal V}(\vartheta)\le0$ contain any already scaled non-box inequalities in $\mathcal V$. The complete direct inequality stack, with every row written in the $\le0$ convention, is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
c_M(p_M)=\col\big(&D_{\rm env}^{-1}g_{\rm env}(y),
D_{\rm eng,red}^{-1}g_{\rm eng,red}[\vartheta,\chi(\vartheta,x,y,\widetilde X_F)],
-D_y^{-1}y,\\
&[X_F^{\min}-\widetilde X_F]/\sigma_F,
D_{\vartheta,{\rm span}}^{-1}(\vartheta^L-\vartheta),
D_{\vartheta,{\rm span}}^{-1}(\vartheta-\vartheta^U),
c_{\mathcal V}(\vartheta)\big)\le0.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Sdirectineq}
\end{equation}
The feed-TSS row is omitted from $g_{\rm eng,red}$ because Equation~\eqref{eq:Sfeedaux} imposes the identical restriction; retaining both would duplicate an active constraint. Define the direct-route objective without overloading its arguments by
\begin{equation}
J_M(p_M;x,\varpi)=J[\vartheta,\chi(\vartheta,x,y,\widetilde X_F);\varpi].
\label{eq:Sdirectobjective}
\end{equation}
Let $\mathcal A_M=\{j:c_{M,j}\ge-\tau_{\rm act}\}$. Set inactive multipliers to zero and recover unrestricted $\eta_M$ and active non-negative $\zeta_{M,\mathcal A_M}$ by minimizing
\begin{equation}
\left\|D_M\left[
\nabla_{p_M}J_M+J_{a_M}^\top\eta_M+
J_{c_M,\mathcal A_M}^\top\zeta_{M,\mathcal A_M}
\right]\right\|_2^2,
\label{eq:Sdirectmultiplier}
\end{equation}
again selecting the minimum-norm multiplier pair among ties. The direct Lagrangian is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_M=J_M(p_M;x,\varpi)+\eta_M^\top a_M(p_M)+\zeta_M^\top c_M(p_M),\qquad
r_{M,{\rm stat}}=\|D_M\nabla_{p_M}\mathcal L_M\|_\infty.
\label{eq:Sdirectlag}
\end{equation}
A direct candidate is called first-order KKT-stationary and feasible only if
\begin{equation}
\|a_M\|_\infty\le10^{-8},\quad
\|\pos{c_M}\|_\infty\le10^{-6},\quad
r_{M,{\rm stat}}\le10^{-6},
\label{eq:Sdirectprimal}
\end{equation}
and the dimensionless dual-feasibility and complementarity residuals
\begin{equation}
\|\pos{-\zeta_M}\|_\infty,\qquad
\|\zeta_M\odot c_M\|_\infty
\label{eq:Sdirectdual}
\end{equation}
are each no greater than $10^{-6}$. These quantities are recalculated from the reported physical variables, rather than copied from a solver status. A primary or permitted recovery endpoint is retained with its provenance when it passes the applicable feasibility audit. If it is validated feasible but stationarity cannot be established, it is explicitly labeled stationarity-unresolved; otherwise the route is a recorded case failure and execution continues. First-order KKT stationarity is not described as proof of local or global optimality.

Whole-test smooth/reference validation is retired and is not an admission gate. The $n_T=3343$ frozen holdout inputs are used for raw and projected surrogate prediction metrics only. Any partial fixed-input smooth/reference rows produced under the legacy protocol remain archived with a retired, excluded-from-analysis status; they do not enter a denominator, gate, or scientific claim.

Instead, every available selected surrogate and direct candidate from the nominal and ten scenario cases is evaluated on one common exact nonsmooth mechanistic reference, for at most 22 candidate decisions. A route that supplies no selected candidate contributes an explicit missing-candidate status rather than an invented decision. Every decision is replayed twice, using the declared primary and alternate influent-based mechanistic initializations and never a smooth optimizer state; both exact starts are retained.

Every exact start must pass the same scaled mass-conservation, non-negativity, domain, residual, and reduced-stability audits used for mechanistic generation. Their roots must satisfy both
\begin{equation}
\|D_{y,{\rm gen}}^{-1}(y_{\rm ref}^{(1)}-y_{\rm ref}^{(2)})\|_\infty\le10^{-6},
\qquad
\|D_y^{-1}(y_{\rm ref}^{(1)}-y_{\rm ref}^{(2)})\|_\infty\le10^{-6}.
\label{eq:Sreplayroots}
\end{equation}
A categorical receiver, storage-capacity, settling-floor, settling-cap, or flux-minimum assignment must agree when both starts are outside their declared ambiguity bands. A mismatch for which either start is boundary-ambiguous is retained as a qualifier when Equation~\eqref{eq:Sreplayroots} and all physical checks pass; boundary ambiguity is not by itself a rejection. Root disagreement, non-ambiguous categorical disagreement, solver failure, or a failed exact physical/stability audit makes the replay invalid. Engineering feasibility is calculated from the accepted exact response and is a separate requirement for route comparison.

For each candidate, the physical ledger has distinct source rows for the raw prediction, projected prediction, optimizer-native response, exact start~1, and exact start~2. The optimizer-native response is the projected state for the surrogate route and the smooth-NLP endpoint for the direct route. Each source records its finite status, maximum scaled mass-conservation residual and exceedance count at $10^{-8}$, and maximum scaled negativity and exceedance count at $10^{-10}$; undefined nonlinear quantities are explicitly marked rather than dropped. Thus a projection pass cannot hide a raw violation, and agreement of objective values cannot hide a mechanistic violation.

Let $J_{k,{\rm nat}}$ and $J_{k,{\rm ref}}$ denote the optimizer-native and exact-reference objectives for route $k\in\{S,M\}$. Each route reports $J_{k,{\rm nat}}-J_{k,{\rm ref}}$ and all objective-component differences whenever its exact replay is valid. The exact objective therefore remains reportable when the replay is engineering-infeasible; engineering failure changes the separate eligibility field rather than erasing a valid reference value. A paired surrogate/direct comparison is eligible only when both candidates exist, both exact replays are valid, and both exact responses are engineering-feasible. It then reports
\begin{equation}
\Delta J_{S-M}=J_{S,{\rm ref}}-J_{M,{\rm ref}},\qquad
r_{J,{\rm sym}}=\frac{\Delta J_{S-M}}
{\max\{1,|J_{S,{\rm ref}}|,|J_{M,{\rm ref}}|\}},
\label{eq:Scommonreferenceobjectives}
\end{equation}
the percentage penalty relative to the direct exact-reference objective, objective-component differences, and the RMS and maximum differences between normalized controls. These quantities compare selected feasible candidates; they are not regret or global-optimality measures. Direct failure leaves an unpaired surrogate result and its exact replay reportable without fabricating a paired metric.

\section{Prediction, decision, and timing metrics}

For response block $\mathcal B$, sample count $n$, and untouched-test prediction method $\mathfrak m\in\{{\rm raw},{\rm proj}\}$, let $\sigma_{\chi,j}=(D_\chi)_{jj}$ and define
\begin{align}
\mathrm{nRMSE}^{(\mathfrak m)}_{\mathcal B}&=\left[\frac1{n|\mathcal B|}\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j\in\mathcal B}
\left(\frac{\chi_{ij}^{(\mathfrak m)}-\chi_{ij}^{\rm ref}}{\sigma_{\chi,j}}\right)^2\right]^{1/2},\\
\mathrm{nMAE}^{(\mathfrak m)}_{\mathcal B}&=\frac1{n|\mathcal B|}\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j\in\mathcal B}
\left|\frac{\chi_{ij}^{(\mathfrak m)}-\chi_{ij}^{\rm ref}}{\sigma_{\chi,j}}\right|.
\end{align}
Bias and $R^2$ are calculated coordinatewise. Blocks are mixer, reactors 1--$N$ separately, overflow flows, underflow flows, layers, complete Clarifier, and complete state. Physical-unit metrics are additionally reported for stage DO/TN/TP/TSS and Clarifier outlet COD/TN/TP/TSS, layer TSS, and inventory.

At each selected decision, prediction error and decision comparison remain distinct. Raw, projected, optimizer-native, and exact-reference states are evaluated wherever defined at both $\vartheta_S$ and $\vartheta_M$. Their scaled error vectors use the same $D_\chi$. The route-specific native/reference objective discrepancy is separate from the signed common-reference candidate difference in Equation~\eqref{eq:Scommonreferenceobjectives}. Neither is called regret because neither optimization route has a global certificate. Branch qualification and convergence qualification are independent reporting dimensions: a valid boundary-qualified exact replay does not confer stationarity, and a convergence certificate does not establish exact-reference validity.

No repeated inference benchmark is run on the 3,343-row frozen holdout block. Computational comparison uses only durations already recorded while executing robustness cases 1--10. For each case, surrogate primary-search time includes the seven-control search and its cold projection-QP evaluations; certification time separately includes the active-set audit, feasible polls, ray-acceleration trials, and final replay. Complete surrogate time is primary search plus certification. Direct primary time covers the three-stage smooth continuation, and complete direct time adds the single conditional recovery when one is actually attempted. Exact-reference replay is reported separately by route. The nominal case is excluded from timing aggregation. For each timing category the report gives the number of available robustness cases, total, arithmetic mean, median, interquartile range, nearest-rank 95th percentile, and maximum. A failed route's expended primary-attempt time remains in the computational denominator even when it supplies no selected candidate. The reported casewise ratio is complete direct-route time divided by complete surrogate-route time, so a value above one favors the surrogate route under these definitions.

\section{Required reporting tables and failure accounting}

The journal record contains:
\begin{enumerate}
\item foldwise ridge scores, selected $\gamma_{\rm R}$, penalized-system condition number, effective degrees of freedom, and coefficient audit;
\item raw and projected test metrics for every response block and coordinate;
\item projection feasibility, correction, leverage, particulate-recovery, reactor-residual, and Clarifier-flux diagnostics;
\item nominal raw, projected, optimizer-native, and exact-mechanistic stage/Clarifier profiles;
\item ten casewise scenario rows for both optimization routes and exact common-reference replays;
\item exact-reference validity and objective, branch qualifier, engineering feasibility, and paired-comparison eligibility for every available selected candidate, retaining valid objectives even when engineering-infeasible;
\item source-specific mass-conservation and non-negativity audits for raw, projected, optimizer-native, and both exact starts when present;
\item solver outcomes, active constraints, objective decomposition, active-set/COBYQA fallback use, strong-KKT or v2 finite-resolution poll tier, direction coverage, diagnostic rank, coarse revalidation, fallback and poll evaluation budgets, direct-recovery provenance, and timing;
\item the eleven superseded v1 poll outcomes and their explicit excluded-from-analysis status;
\item a generation-attempt table with $m_D$ and $m_T$ denominators, acceptance/rejection reasons, supplemental rounds, and candidate-to-final-slot provenance; and
\item an optimization failure table whose denominator includes every nominal or sensitivity case.
\end{enumerate}
An unobserved failure class is retained with count zero. Optimization, convergence, exact-reference validity, engineering feasibility, branch qualification, and paired-comparison eligibility are recorded as separate status axes rather than collapsed into one favorable outcome. A branch-boundary ambiguity is a qualifier, not a failure class. Candidate absence, projection failure, incomplete poll or exhaustion, absence of any feasible nonzero poll displacement, primary-direct failure, recovery failure or unavailability, exact integration failure, root disagreement, physical or reduced-stability failure, and engineering infeasibility remain explicit. Feasible-direction rank is diagnostic and cannot alone fail v2. Downstream diagnostics, including a valid exact objective from an engineering-infeasible response, are retained whenever finite even after a failed check.
