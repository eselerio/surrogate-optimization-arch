\begin{abstract}
This supplement specifies the mechanistic generator, sampling design, extended-ICSOR fit, system-wide invariant-constraint projection, optimization protocol, exact-reference replay, and reporting rules. The comparison contains two routes: extended ICSOR ($S$) and the unchanged smooth mechanistic nonlinear program ($M$). Extended ICSOR retains the second-order regression of the ICSOR surrogate of \citet{Selerio2026} and applies its invariant-constraint projection to the complete recycling-system response, including a feed-dependent non-settleable-solids floor on overflow TSS.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and coordinate contract}

The plant contains $N$ completely mixed biological reactors and an $L$-layer mechanistic Clarifier. There are $F=F_S+F_X$ components and $K$ independent stoichiometric invariants. Extended ICSOR uses the reduced response
\begin{equation}
\chi=\col(m,c_1,\ldots,c_N,g_E,g_U,M_{\rm cl})\in\R^{n_\chi},\qquad n_\chi=(N+3)F+1,
\label{eq:Sresponse}
\end{equation}
where $m$ is the mixer concentration, $c_i$ is the reactor-$i$ concentration, $g_E=q_Ec_E$ and $g_U=q_Uc_U$ are Clarifier outlet component flows on the fresh-flow basis, and $M_{\rm cl}$ is the aggregate Clarifier solids inventory. The full mechanistic state is $y=\col(c_1,\ldots,c_N,s_1,\ldots,s_L)\in\R_+^{NF+L}$. Internal Clarifier layers are retained by the mechanistic route and reference replay, but are not predicted by the surrogate.

\section{Mechanistic generator and reduced response}

Using fresh flow $Q_0$ as the basis, $q_P=1+r_I+r_R$, $q_C=1+r_R$, $q_U=r_R+w$, and $q_E=1-w$. The mixer balance is
\begin{equation}
q_Pm=x+r_Ic_N+r_Rc_U=x+r_Ic_N+(r_R/q_U)g_U.
\label{eq:Smixer}
\end{equation}
For reactor $i$, with $c_0=m$ and $d_i=24q_P/h_i$, the steady balance is
\begin{equation}
0=d_i(c_{i-1}-c_i)+\nu^\top\rho_i(c_i;\vartheta)+\omega_i(a_i,c_i)e_{S_O}+\xi_i(\vartheta).
\label{eq:Sreactor}
\end{equation}
The invariant operator $A\in\R^{K\times F}$ satisfies $A\nu^\top=0$ and $Ae_{S_O}=0$, hence
\begin{equation}
A(c_i-c_{i-1})=d_i^{-1}A\xi_i(\vartheta).
\label{eq:Sinvariants}
\end{equation}
The Clarifier component balance and soluble pass-through are $q_Cc_N=g_E+g_U$ and $E_Sg_U=q_UE_Sc_N$. Particulate densification requires $E_Xg_U\ge q_UE_Xc_N$. Endpoint TSS is recovered from the outlet flows: $X_E=t_X^\top g_E/q_E$ and $X_U=t_X^\top g_U/q_U$.

For solids concentration $s_\ell$ and Clarifier-feed TSS $X_{\rm feed}=t_X^\top c_N$, the exact Tak\'acs settling relation used by the generator has the form
\begin{align}
\Delta_\ell&=s_\ell-f_{\rm ns}X_{\rm feed},\notag\\
v_s(s_\ell;X_{\rm feed})&=\max\!\left\{0,\min\!\left[v_{\max},v_0\left(e^{-r_h\Delta_\ell}-e^{-r_p\Delta_\ell}\right)\right]\right\},
\label{eq:Ssettling}
\end{align}
where $r_p>r_h>0$. Thus the positive-part operation assigns zero settling velocity at and below $f_{\rm ns}X_{\rm feed}$. The reduced response does not contain the layers or their fluxes, so the projection declares the corresponding conservative endpoint closure
\begin{equation}
X_E\ge f_{\rm ns}X_{\rm feed},
\quad\text{equivalently}\quad
q_Ef_{\rm ns}t_X^\top c_N-t_X^\top g_E\le0.
\label{eq:Sfloor}
\end{equation}
The case value is $f_{\rm ns}=0.00228$. Equation~\eqref{eq:Sfloor} rules out a zero or near-zero surrogate effluent-solids prediction when feed solids are positive; it does not reproduce Equation~\eqref{eq:Ssettling} or establish a valid layer profile \citep{Takacs1991}.

The aggregate inventory is $M_{\rm cl}=\sum_{\ell=1}^L V_{{\rm cl},\ell}s_\ell$. Eliminating unreported layers gives
\begin{align}
M_{\rm cl}^{-}&=(V_{\rm cl}-V_{{\rm cl},L})X_E+V_{{\rm cl},L}X_U,\notag\\
M_{\rm cl}^{+}&=V_{{\rm cl},1}X_E+(V_{\rm cl}-V_{{\rm cl},1})X_U,\qquad
M_{\rm cl}^{-}\le M_{\rm cl}\le M_{\rm cl}^{+}.
\label{eq:Sinventory}
\end{align}
This interval guarantees only a profile compatible with the endpoint envelope. The nonlinear settling-flux equations and detailed layer profile remain mechanistic quantities.

The generator uses ASM2d with two-step nitrification, five named invariants, five equal-volume reactors, and ten 0.4-m Clarifier layers. A candidate is retained only after both declared steady-state starts satisfy finite-state, conservation, non-negativity, domain, layer-residual, and reduced-stability audits. The full state is then deterministically mapped to Equation~\eqref{eq:Sresponse}.

\section{Sampling, partitioning, and model fit}

The stopped 50,000-target design produced 18,211 completed attempts, of which 16,714 were accepted. Accepted rows are ordered by original candidate index into 13,371 development rows and 3,343 frozen post-selection holdout rows. Ten influent-only robustness vectors are generated by a 20-dimensional Latin hypercube with seed 314159. These ten vectors are post-selection stress cases, not independent validation data.

For the seven controls in the case study, the extended-ICSOR input is $z=\col[D_{\vartheta,\rm fit}^{-1}(\vartheta-\bar\vartheta),D_{x,\rm fit}^{-1}(x-\bar x)]\in\R^{27}$. Its complete second-order feature vector is
\begin{equation}
\phi_S(\vartheta,x)=\col[1,D_{r,\rm fit}^{-1}\{\col[z,\vech(zz^\top)]-\bar r\}]\in\R^{406}.
\label{eq:Sfeatures}
\end{equation}
With development response mean $\bar\chi$, positive response scale $D_\chi$, and ridge coefficient matrix $\widehat C_{S,\gamma_S}$,
\begin{equation}
\chi_{{\rm raw},S}(\vartheta,x)=\bar\chi+D_\chi\widehat C_{S,\gamma_S}\phi_S(\vartheta,x).
\label{eq:Sraw}
\end{equation}
The ridge grid is evaluated in plant-grouped five-fold cross-validation. All coordinates from one mechanistic plant state remain in its assigned fold. The selected penalty is the largest candidate within one standard error of the lowest mean fold error. Final scales and coefficients are refit using all development rows. The holdout is used once for descriptive raw and projected error summaries and never changes features, scales, penalty selection, trust limits, or model fit.

\section{Extended ICSOR system-wide invariant projection}

Extended ICSOR retains the second-order regression of the original ICSOR surrogate \citep{Selerio2026}. Its revision is the system-wide scope of the invariant-constraint projection: the projection operates after the complete response in Equation~\eqref{eq:Sresponse} has been predicted, rather than on an isolated response.

Let $\mathcal H(\vartheta)\chi=\mathfrak b(\vartheta,x)$ collect Equation~\eqref{eq:Smixer}, all invariant transitions in Equation~\eqref{eq:Sinvariants}, the Clarifier component balance, and soluble pass-through. Let $\mathcal G(\vartheta)\chi\le0$ collect particulate densification, Equation~\eqref{eq:Sfloor}, and the division-free form of Equation~\eqref{eq:Sinventory}. Therefore $n_g=F_X+3$ and, including response non-negativity, $n_q=n_\chi+n_g$. At fixed $(\vartheta,x)$ the projected response is
\begin{align}
\widehat u_S={}&\arg\min_u\ \tfrac12\|u\|_2^2\notag\\
\text{subject to }&D_b^{-1}\{\mathcal H[\chi_{{\rm raw},S}+D_\chi u]-\mathfrak b\}=0,\notag\\
&\chi_{{\rm raw},S}+D_\chi u\ge0,\qquad
D_g^{-1}\mathcal G[\chi_{{\rm raw},S}+D_\chi u]\le0,\notag\\
\widehat\chi_S={}&\chi_{{\rm raw},S}+D_\chi\widehat u_S.
\label{eq:SQP}
\end{align}
The identity Hessian makes this projection unique whenever the feasible set is nonempty. $D_b$ and $D_g$ are positive development-frozen physical row scales; $D_\chi$ is the response scale used only for the projection metric. The new floor row uses the same development-frozen RMS term-scale convention as every other inequality row, so its scale improves conditioning without changing Equation~\eqref{eq:Sfloor}. Thus the QP makes the smallest standardized response change while preserving the declared physical feasible set.

Every QP is cold-solved and independently audited for equality feasibility, inequality feasibility, non-negativity, dual feasibility, stationarity, complementarity, and primal--dual gap. Apart from the scalar endpoint floor in Equation~\eqref{eq:Sfloor}, the projection does not impose biological kinetics, nonlinear Clarifier settling fluxes, dynamic stability, or a layer profile. Those properties are assessed only in smooth-direct or exact reference states.

The development-frozen extended-ICSOR trust constraints limit standardized projection correction, whole-system regularized feature leverage, particulate-recovery deviation, and smooth reactor-balance residual. Their thresholds are nearest-rank 95th percentiles of the applicable grouped out-of-fold development diagnostics. The frozen admission gate also requires finite, audited out-of-fold projections and full-response and inventory-coordinate raw nRMSE below one.

\section{Optimization and reference adjudication}

The case controls are $\vartheta=(H,a_3,a_4,a_5,r_I,r_R,w)^\top$. Both routes use identical bounds, objective priorities, engineering constraints, and nominal influent. Extended ICSOR minimizes the shared engineering objective subject to the operating box, engineering constraints, its frozen trust constraints, and Equation~\eqref{eq:SQP}. The QP is cold-solved at every distinct trial control. When active-set sensitivity passes rank, conditioning, strict-complementarity, and finite-difference checks, it supports the strong first-order KKT audit. Otherwise deterministic value-only COBYQA is used, followed by complete feasible no-descent polls at normalized radii $10^{-3}$ and $10^{-4}$. This finite-resolution result is not a local or global optimality certificate.

The smooth mechanistic NLP retains the full reactor and Clarifier state, smooth kinetic and settling regularizations, the same seven controls, and the same objective and engineering requirements. Its primary solve uses the unchanged three-stage smoothing continuation from the box center. If this primary solve fails, one conditional recovery may begin from the certified extended-ICSOR control. No recovery is made after a successful primary solve.

Every available selected decision is replayed from two declared starts on the exact nonsmooth, layer-resolved mechanistic model. A replay must pass root-agreement, mass-conservation, non-negativity, non-settleable-floor, layer-residual, domain, reduced-stability, and engineering audits to be eligible for the $S$--$M$ comparison. Exact objectives remain reported even for an engineering-infeasible replay, but such a replay cannot enter the paired comparison.

\section{Prediction, decision, timing, and reporting}

For every reduced-response block, raw and projected extended-ICSOR nRMSE, nMAE, bias, and $R^2$ are reported on grouped out-of-fold development rows and on the descriptive holdout. Physical-unit reports include stage DO, COD, TN, TP, and TSS; Clarifier overflow and underflow quality; and $M_{\rm cl}$. For Equation~\eqref{eq:Sfloor}, raw and projected violation counts, maximum violation, minimum slack, and projected active-row fraction are also reported. Layer-TSS metrics are reported only for smooth-direct and exact mechanistic profiles.

At both selected decisions, extended-ICSOR raw and projected responses, the optimizer-native response, and the exact-reference reduced response are kept distinct. The signed exact-reference difference is
\begin{equation}
\Delta J_{S-M}=J(\vartheta_S,\chi_{{\rm ref},S})-J(\vartheta_M,\chi_{{\rm ref},M}).
\end{equation}
It is reported only when both exact replays are valid and engineering feasible. The nominal case and each of the ten robustness cases retain a record for both routes, including failures and unresolved convergence tiers.

Timing aggregation excludes the nominal case and holdout-wide inference. Robustness-case records report primary and complete solve time, QP evaluations, fallback and poll work, exact replay time, and the ratio $t_M/t_S$ when both complete route times are available. Failed attempts remain in the corresponding time and case denominators.

\section{Required reporting and failure accounting}

The record contains the accepted/rejected generation ledger; grouped-fold and holdout prediction summaries; extended-ICSOR coefficient and projection audits, including non-settleable-floor slack and activity; trust diagnostics; selected controls; raw, projected, optimizer-native, and exact responses; exact-reference validity and engineering-feasibility status; $S$--$M$ comparison eligibility; source-specific physical audits; solver and convergence records; and casewise timing summaries. Optimization failure, convergence qualification, exact-reference validity, engineering feasibility, and pairwise-comparison eligibility are distinct status fields. A finite result that fails a scientific threshold is retained as a diagnostic; missing or non-finite arrays, an undefined scale or feature map, a failed factorization, or an integrity/checkpoint inconsistency remains a hard computational failure.
