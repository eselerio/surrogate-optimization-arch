\begin{abstract}
Activated sludge design and operation couple an inlet mixer, staged biological conversion, mixed-liquor recycle, return activated sludge, sludge wasting, and secondary clarification. Repeated solution of this closed mixer--reactor--Clarifier network is too costly for a large bounded search. This study develops a bilevel formulation for a prescribed $N$-stage activated sludge system with a Clarifier; $N$ is a topology parameter, and the case study fixes $N=5$. A dimensionless second-order statistical surrogate predicts the inlet mixer, every reactor outlet, the Clarifier outlet component mass flows, and the ten-layer solids profile. A strictly convex lower-level quadratic program then returns one unique corrected network state satisfying recycle mixing, reactor stoichiometric invariants, Clarifier component balances, soluble pass-through, particulate clarification and densification directions, layer-inventory consistency, and non-negativity. The upper level jointly selects reactor volume through nominal hydraulic retention time and selects aeration, mixed-liquor recycle, return-sludge flow, and waste-sludge flow. Training uses a fixed, full-rank standardized multiresponse least-squares estimator on a unique quadratic basis. A 4,000-state set held out from the development fit must pass predictive, numerical, and correction-reliance criteria before the 20,000-state production refit is frozen. The complete mechanistic, training, deployment, numerical-acceptance, and validation definitions are contained in this article. Mechanistic comparison uses a coupled ASM2d-TSN--multilayer-Clarifier model and distinguishes prediction fidelity from the finite-budget mechanistic incumbent gap. The result is a process-level surrogate whose stated physical guarantees and limitations can be traced stream by stream through the activated sludge system.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item One surrogate represents the complete recycling $N$-stage activated sludge system.
\item A unique scaled-$L_2$ QP enforces mixer, reactor, and Clarifier physics.
\item Ten Clarifier-layer solids states make the SRT inventory physically traceable.
\item A full-rank scaled quadratic basis gives one fixed multiresponse fit.
\item Mechanistic simulations separate prediction fidelity from decision quality.
\end{highlights}

\section{Introduction}
Activated sludge plants are recycling systems. Mixed liquor may be returned to an upstream anoxic zone to make oxidized nitrogen available for denitrification, while settled biomass is returned from the Clarifier to maintain the biological inventory. A waste stream controls solids age, and the Clarifier overflow is the treated discharge. These streams are familiar individually, but changing one changes the hydraulic residence time, solids loading, and composition seen elsewhere in the loop.

Detailed activated sludge models represent these interactions through nonlinear reaction rates and clarification fluxes. They are indispensable for process analysis, yet repeated closed-loop steady-state solution can dominate the cost of optimization (de Araujo et al., 2013). Surrogate models can reduce that cost (Bhosekar and Ierapetritou, 2018), and component-space constraints can preserve mass conservation and non-negativity in activated-sludge regression (Selerio Jr., 2026). A complete recycling plant creates a broader risk: the optimizer can seek a small negative concentration, a broken recycle balance, or a favorable solids inventory that would never occur in the plant.

The recycle topology adds a second difficulty. If the reactors and Clarifier are predicted independently, their responses must be iterated around the MLR and RAS loops. A unique answer from each unit does not imply a unique closed-loop fixed point. The complete plant state is therefore represented by one system-level response and reconciled by one explicitly physical calculation. The response uses a unique-monomial quadratic basis and a single coefficient matrix whose identifiability is checked directly from the standardized design.

The resulting contribution is a process-level bilevel model (Colson et al., 2007). The lower level is a strictly convex projection that has one answer for every admissible flow vector. The upper level compares feasible design-and-operation candidates using clarified effluent quality, reactor volume, aeration, pumping, and wasted-solids terms. The Clarifier solids inventory is calculated from predicted layer concentrations. The article defines the mechanistic generator, estimator, projection, trust screens, search, and validation route as one self-contained calculation.

The mixer--reactor--Clarifier description covers a prescribed number $N$ of biological stages; $N$ is not an integer decision in the present optimization. The derivation applies to any fixed $N$, whereas the case study fixes five equal-volume CSTRs, two unaerated followed by three aerated. In the following sections, the equations are introduced in the order that an engineer can follow the water: fresh feed and recycles meet, mixed liquor passes through the reactors, solids and liquid divide in the Clarifier, and only then is the response scored.

\section{Theory and Calculation}

\subsection{Walking Through the Recycling Process}

The combined stream passes once through the $N$ CSTRs. MLR is removed from the last-stage outlet before clarification; the remaining mixed liquor enters the Clarifier. Clarifier underflow is split into RAS and WAS without another composition-changing step, so RAS and WAS share the state $c_U$.

The fresh flow $Q_0$ is a convenient common ruler for the three manipulated side streams. Define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:flow_ratios}
r_I=\frac{Q_I}{Q_0},\qquad
r_R=\frac{Q_R}{Q_0},\qquad
w=\frac{Q_W}{Q_0},
\end{equation}
where $r_I$ is the MLR ratio, $r_R$ is the RAS ratio, and $w$ is the WAS ratio. The domain requires $r_I\geq0$, $r_R>0$, and $0\leq w<1$. These restrictions are not cosmetic: they keep every outlet flow and every concentration recovery division positive.

Starting at the mixer and following the arrows in Figure~\ref{fig:process_schematic} gives four dimensionless throughflows:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:network_flows}
\underbrace{q_P}_{\text{through CSTRs}}=1+r_I+r_R,\qquad
\underbrace{q_C}_{\text{into Clarifier}}=1+r_R,
\qquad
\underbrace{q_U}_{\text{underflow}}=r_R+w,
\qquad
\underbrace{q_E}_{\text{effluent}}=1-w.
\end{equation}
The first identity counts fresh feed plus both returns. Removing MLR from the last-stage outlet leaves $q_CQ_0$. The Clarifier then divides that stream into $q_EQ_0$ and $q_UQ_0$, and the definitions show directly that $q_C=q_E+q_U$. This small hydraulic calculation is used repeatedly below; it is what makes RAS an internal stream and WAS an external loss.

Let $m$ denote the mixer outlet, $c_i$ the outlet of CSTR $i$, and $c_U$ the Clarifier underflow. The mass entering the first tank must equal the fresh-feed mass plus the two returned masses, component by component:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:headworks_mixing}
q_Pm=x+r_Ic_N+r_Rc_U.
\end{equation}
Every term has the form ``flow relative to $Q_0$ times concentration.'' The factor $Q_0$ therefore cancels. Equation~\eqref{eq:headworks_mixing} is an identity, not a relationship to be learned. A topology with MLR withdrawn from another stage uses that stage state in place of $c_N$.

For a general prescribed topology, collect the stage volumes, aeration settings, and recycle decisions as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:general_operating_vector}
\vartheta=
\left(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_N,
a_1,\ldots,a_N,r_I,r_R,w\right)^\top\in\mathcal V,
\qquad
\tau_i=\frac{24V_i}{Q_0}.
\end{equation}
Here $\tau_i$ is the nominal HRT in hours that tank $i$ would provide at fresh flow alone. At fixed $Q_0$, choosing $\tau_i$ chooses volume, $V_i=Q_0\tau_i/24$; it is therefore a design decision, not merely an operating knob. The liquid actually traverses the tank at $q_PQ_0$, so its one-pass residence time and dilution rate are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:recycle_hydraulics}
h_i=\frac{\tau_i}{q_P},
\qquad
d_i=\frac{24q_P}{\tau_i}\ \mathrm{d}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
An engineer can read Equation~\eqref{eq:recycle_hydraulics} directly: increasing either recycle while tank volume is fixed moves more water through the same vessel and shortens one pass. The surrogate must see this coupling because the biology does.

\subsection{From CSTR Balances to Reaction-Free Inventory Rails}

The next task is to carry physically meaningful information through all $N$ biological stages without asking the fast surrogate to reproduce every nonlinear rate. For CSTR $i$, steady accumulation is zero, so the difference between outlet and inlet transport must be supplied by reactions and oxygen transfer:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stage_balance}
d_i(c_i-c_{i-1})=\nu^\top\varrho_i+\omega_i e_{S_O},
\qquad c_0=m.
\end{equation}
The $F$ entries of $c_i-c_{i-1}$ are component changes across the tank. The $P$ entries of $\varrho_i$ are the ASM2d-TSN process rates, and the columns of $\nu^\top$ translate those rates into component production or consumption. The last term adds oxygen through aeration; $e_{S_O}$ selects only the dissolved-oxygen coordinate.

The rate vector is precisely the expensive, nonlinear object that should not be carried into every surrogate evaluation. What is needed instead is a set of combinations of components that no modeled reaction or oxygen transfer can change. Appendix~\ref{app:stoichiometry} constructs five physical conservation rows in $B$ and normalizes each row separately to form $A\in\R^{K\times F}$. Separate normalization keeps the inert-soluble, nitrogen, phosphorus, alkalinity, and metal inventories as recognizable rails. Its required checks are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:invariant_conditions}
A\nu^\top=0,\qquad Ae_{S_O}=0,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(A)=K,\qquad \|A_{k,:}\|_2=1.
\end{equation}
The first equality says that each row of $A$ annihilates every reaction source; the second prevents external oxygen transfer from masquerading as a conserved inventory; full row rank removes duplication, and the final relation fixes each rail's scale without mixing it with another inventory.

Premultiplying Equation~\eqref{eq:stage_balance} by $A$ removes both source terms. Because $d_i>0$, the remaining statement is simply
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stage_invariants}
Ac_1=Am,\qquad
Ac_i=Ac_{i-1},\quad i=2,\ldots,N.
\end{equation}
These equalities are useful to picture as $K$ rails extending through the train: individual COD, nitrogen, phosphorus, and biomass components may move between forms, but the selected stoichiometric inventories cannot jump from one tank to the next. They need not equal $Ax$, because RAS changes the material circulating inside the plant. The fresh-feed relation appears only after the external Clarifier and WAS boundaries are closed.

\subsection{Following Components Through the Clarifier}

The Clarifier changes where material leaves, not how much of a non-reactive component exists. Its component balance is therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clarifier_balance_concentration}
q_Cc_N=q_Ec_E+q_Uc_U.
\end{equation}
The small underflow ratio can make $c_U$ numerically large even when its component mass is ordinary. It is more stable and more intuitive to predict the outlet masses normalized by fresh flow:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clarifier_mass_flow_coordinates}
g_E=q_Ec_E,\qquad g_U=q_Uc_U.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:clarifier_balance_concentration} then becomes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clarifier_balance}
g_E+g_U=q_Cc_N.
\end{equation}
Once $q_E$ and $q_U$ are known, the outlet concentrations are recovered uniquely as $c_E=g_E/q_E$ and $c_U=g_U/q_U$. Thus, non-negative mass-flow coordinates also give non-negative outlet concentrations.

Clarification must not be represented as solids generation. The TSS map satisfies $t_X\in\R_+^F$, $E_St_X=0$, and $\|t_X\|_1>0$. For a particulate component $j$ whose Clarifier-feed mass exceeds the denominator tolerance defined in the numerical contract, define the underflow recovery and concentration ratio by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:component_recovery}
\eta_j=\frac{g_{U,j}}{q_Cc_{N,j}},
\qquad
\delta_j=\frac{c_{U,j}}{c_{N,j}}
=\eta_j\frac{q_C}{q_U}.
\end{equation}
The first factor, $\eta_j$, is the fraction of incoming particulate mass sent downward. Non-negativity and Clarifier balance give $0\leq\eta_j\leq1$; the deployed densification rule sharpens this to $q_U/q_C\leq\eta_j\leq1$ and therefore $1\leq\delta_j\leq q_C/q_U$. The factor $q_C/q_U$ expresses how recovered mass is concentrated into a smaller water stream. When the feed denominator is below tolerance, neither ratio is identifiable and it is reported as unavailable.

The soluble and particulate coordinates require different physical rules. Let $E_S$ select the ten soluble components and $E_X$ the ten particulate components. Ideal soluble pass-through means that the underflow carries the same soluble concentration as the feed:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:soluble_passthrough}
E_Sg_U=q_UE_Sc_N.
\end{equation}
Together with Equation~\eqref{eq:clarifier_balance}, this also gives $E_Sc_E=E_Sc_U=E_Sc_N$. For particulates, the mechanistic Clarifier moves a common floc solids flux. The convex correction enforces the physically necessary direction of that movement component by component:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:particulate_densification}
E_Xg_U\geq q_UE_Xc_N.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, every particulate underflow concentration is at least its feed concentration. Because $g_E\geq0$ and Equation~\eqref{eq:clarifier_balance} holds, recovery cannot exceed the incoming particulate mass. The mechanistic common-recovery relation is evaluated as a diagnostic; it is not falsely claimed as an exact linear-QP guarantee.

A single learned Clarifier inventory would still leave the optimizer a loophole: that scalar could change SRT without being tied to any layer. The state is therefore made more physical by predicting the $N_\ell$ layer TSS concentrations
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clarifier_layer_vector}
s=(s_1,\ldots,s_{N_\ell})^\top,
\end{equation}
ordered from the top water surface to the bottom hopper. The two end layers are tied to the outlet TSS values,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clarifier_endpoint_tss}
q_Es_1=t_X^\top g_E,
\qquad
q_Us_{N_\ell}=t_X^\top g_U,
\end{equation}
and each interior layer is kept within the physically interpretable top-to-bottom envelope,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clarifier_layer_envelope}
s_1\leq s_\ell\leq s_{N_\ell},
\qquad \ell=2,\ldots,N_\ell-1.
\end{equation}
The Clarifier solids inventory is now a consequence of a pictured profile rather than an independent output:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clarifier_inventory}
M_{\mathrm{cl}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_\ell}V_{\mathrm{cl},\ell}s_\ell,
\qquad
\overline M_{\mathrm{cl}}=\frac{M_{\mathrm{cl}}}{Q_0}.
\end{equation}

The stream bookkeeping can now be closed around the entire plant. Premultiply the mixer balance by $A$, use the reactor rails in Equation~\eqref{eq:stage_invariants}, substitute the Clarifier balance, and cancel MLR and RAS because both remain inside the boundary. What remains is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:plant_boundary_invariant}
Ax=q_EAc_E+wAc_U
=Ag_E+\frac{w}{q_U}Ag_U.
\end{equation}
This is the engineer's external check: conserved inventory enters only with fresh wastewater and leaves only with treated overflow and wasted sludge.

\subsection{One Identifiable Response for the Complete Loop}

Separate unit surrogates would have to be iterated around two recycle paths. Instead, collect every quantity needed to close those paths in one vector:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stacked_state}
\chi=
\begin{bmatrix}
m\\c_1\\\vdots\\c_N\\g_E\\g_U\\s
\end{bmatrix}
\in\R^L,
\qquad
L=(N+3)F+N_\ell.
\end{equation}
For the five-CSTR, 20-component, ten-layer case, $L=170$. Concentrations, normalized mass flows, and layer TSS have different units, so the regression is built in standardized coordinates. Let
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:standardized_inputs_states}
\widetilde\vartheta=S_\vartheta^{-1}(\vartheta-\mu_\vartheta),\qquad
\widetilde x=S_x^{-1}(x-\mu_x),\qquad
\widetilde\chi=D_\chi^{-1}(\chi-\mu_\chi),
\end{equation}
where all centers and positive diagonal scales come only from the applicable fitting rows.

A second-order model needs squares and cross-products, but it needs each monomial only once. Let $q(v)$ list $v_jv_k$ for $j\leq k$, and form the nonconstant vector
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:unique_quadratic_terms}
\psi(\vartheta,x)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\widetilde\vartheta\\
\widetilde x\\
q(\widetilde\vartheta)\\
q(\widetilde x)\\
\widetilde\vartheta\otimes\widetilde x
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Centering and scaling these nonconstant terms once more gives the serialized feature vector
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:system_feature_map}
\phi(\vartheta,x)=
\begin{bmatrix}
1\\S_\psi^{-1}[\psi(\vartheta,x)-\mu_\psi]
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
d_\phi=1+n_\vartheta+F+\frac{n_\vartheta(n_\vartheta+1)}2+\frac{F(F+1)}2+n_\vartheta F.
\end{equation}
For $n_\vartheta=5$ and $F=20$, the basis contains $d_\phi=351$ columns. It includes every linear term, square, within-vector cross-product, and design--influent interaction exactly once. Within $q(v)$ the pairs are serialized lexicographically as $(1,1),(1,2),\ldots,(1,d),(2,2),\ldots,(d,d)$; the Kronecker block is serialized with the influent index varying fastest.

The frozen raw prediction is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:raw_surrogate}
\widetilde\chi_{\mathrm{raw}}=\widehat C\phi(\vartheta,x),
\qquad
\chi_{\mathrm{raw}}=\mu_\chi+D_\chi\widetilde\chi_{\mathrm{raw}}.
\end{equation}
The matrix $\widehat C\in\R^{L\times d_\phi}$ is the complete input--response map. Its design matrix must pass the full-column-rank and condition-number tests stated in the training procedure. For a fixed influent, Equation~\eqref{eq:raw_surrogate} is a vector-valued quadratic in the design and operating decisions.

\subsection{Assembling the Plant Rules into Matrices}

The equations developed stream by stream can now be assembled without hiding their physical order. Define the equality residual by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:network_equality_operator}
\mathcal H(\vartheta)\chi-b(x)=
\begin{bmatrix}
q_Pm-r_Ic_N-(r_R/q_U)g_U-x\\
A(c_1-m)\\
A(c_2-c_1)\\
\vdots\\
A(c_N-c_{N-1})\\
g_E+g_U-q_Cc_N\\
E_Sg_U-q_UE_Sc_N\\
q_Es_1-t_X^\top g_E\\
q_Us_{N_\ell}-t_X^\top g_U
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is $b(x)=(x^\top,0_{R-F}^\top)^\top$. Within every component block the order is Equation~\eqref{eq:component_order}; within every invariant block the row order is the fixed row order of $A$. Thus,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:equality_row_count}
R=F+NK+F+F_S+2,
\end{equation}
where $F_S=10$ is the number of soluble coordinates. The case has $R=77$: rows 1--20 are mixer balances, 21--45 are the five stage-invariant blocks, 46--65 are Clarifier component balances, 66--75 are soluble-pass-through balances, and rows 76--77 are the two TSS endpoints.

The particulate and layer relations have the equally explicit order
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:network_inequality_operator}
\mathcal G(\vartheta)\chi=
\begin{bmatrix}
q_UE_Xc_N-E_Xg_U\\
s_1\mathbf1_{N_\ell-2}-(s_2,\ldots,s_{N_\ell-1})^\top\\
(s_2,\ldots,s_{N_\ell-1})^\top-s_{N_\ell}\mathbf1_{N_\ell-2}
\end{bmatrix}\leq0.
\end{equation}
Here $R_g=F_X+2(N_\ell-2)=26$, with $F_X=10$. Rows 1--10 follow particulate-component order, rows 11--18 impose the top bounds for layers 2--9, and rows 19--26 impose the bottom bounds in the same layer order. Positive diagonal matrices $D_b$ and $D_g$, defined from named physical terms in the training section, scale these rows without changing either feasible set.

An equality-only projection is useful when diagnosing how much of a raw error came from broken network closure. With $D_\chi=\diag(s_{\chi,1},\ldots,s_{\chi,L})$ and every $s_{\chi,j}>0$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:network_affine_program}
\begin{aligned}
\chi_{\mathrm{aff}}={}&
\argminop_{\chi\in\R^L}\ \frac12
\left\|D_\chi^{-1}(\chi-\chi_{\mathrm{raw}})\right\|_2^2\\
\text{subject to}\quad&\mathcal H(\vartheta)\chi=b(x).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Its closed form is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:network_affine_closed}
\chi_{\mathrm{aff}}=
\chi_{\mathrm{raw}}-D_\chi^2\mathcal H^\top
(\mathcal H D_\chi^2\mathcal H^\top)^{-1}
[\mathcal H\chi_{\mathrm{raw}}-b(x)].
\end{equation}
The displayed inverse explains the geometry; the calculation uses linear solves. This diagnostic is evaluated for assessment, but it need not be solved before every optimization QP.

\subsection{The Unique Physical Correction}

The raw response may break an equality, cross zero, or reverse the expected direction of particulate clarification. To correct all such defects at once and to avoid a poorly scaled Hessian, write a candidate physical state as $\chi=\chi_{\mathrm{raw}}+D_\chi u$, where $u$ is a dimensionless displacement. The deployed state is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:network_lower_qp}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat u={}&\argminop_{u\in\R^L}\ \frac12\|u\|_2^2\\
\text{subject to}\quad
&D_b^{-1}\{\mathcal H(\vartheta)[\chi_{\mathrm{raw}}+D_\chi u]-b(x)\}=0,\\
&\chi_{\mathrm{raw}}+D_\chi u\geq0,\\
&D_g^{-1}\mathcal G(\vartheta)[\chi_{\mathrm{raw}}+D_\chi u]\leq0.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
After the QP is solved, the deployed state is defined by
\begin{equation}
\widehat\chi(\vartheta;x)=\chi_{\mathrm{raw}}+D_\chi\widehat u.
\end{equation}
The identity Hessian makes Equation~\eqref{eq:network_lower_qp} strictly convex. Its feasibility can be seen without relying on a numerical solver: choose $m=c_1=\cdots=c_N=x$, $g_E=q_Ex$, $g_U=q_Ux$, and $s_1=\cdots=s_{N_\ell}=t_X^\top x$. This no-conversion point satisfies every equality and inequality. The feasible set is therefore nonempty, and strict convexity gives one unique complete response.

For any equality-feasible $\chi$, weighted Pythagorean decomposition gives
\begin{equation}
\left\|D_\chi^{-1}(\chi-\chi_{\mathrm{raw}})\right\|_2^2
=\left\|D_\chi^{-1}(\chi-\chi_{\mathrm{aff}})\right\|_2^2+\text{constant}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, the direct QP in Equation~\eqref{eq:network_lower_qp} has the same solution as a QP centered on $\chi_{\mathrm{aff}}$, while avoiding a redundant projection during every upper-level evaluation.

The exact deployment contract can now be stated without overstating it. To numerical tolerance, the returned state satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:network_guarantee}
\begin{gathered}
q_P\widehat m=x+r_I\widehat c_N+r_R\widehat c_U,\\
A\widehat c_1=A\widehat m,\qquad
A\widehat c_i=A\widehat c_{i-1},\quad i=2,\ldots,N,\\
\widehat g_E+\widehat g_U=q_C\widehat c_N,\qquad
E_S\widehat c_E=E_S\widehat c_U=E_S\widehat c_N,\\
E_X\widehat c_U\geq E_X\widehat c_N,\qquad
E_X\widehat c_E\leq E_X\widehat c_N,\\
q_E\widehat s_1=t_X^\top\widehat g_E,\qquad
q_U\widehat s_{N_\ell}=t_X^\top\widehat g_U,\\
\widehat s_1\leq\widehat s_\ell\leq\widehat s_{N_\ell},\quad
\ell=2,\ldots,N_\ell-1,\qquad \widehat\chi\geq0.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
These are network, inventory, and direction guarantees. They do not assert that the projected point satisfies every ASM kinetic equation or every nonlinear Clarifier flux relation. Those stronger claims require the independent mechanistic evaluation described later.

\subsection{From a Physical State to an Engineering Decision}

The discharge is $c_E$, not the mixed liquor $c_N$. Let $T$ select the effluent components or composites to be scored, let $D_T$ contain frozen positive production-response scales, and let $\alpha_Q\geq0$ with $\mathbf1^\top\alpha_Q=1$. A dimensionless concentration-quality term is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:quality_objective}
J_Q(\chi)=\alpha_Q^\top D_T^{-1}Tc_E
=\alpha_Q^\top D_T^{-1}T\frac{g_E}{q_E}.
\end{equation}
Concentration is used because the primary case represents discharge-quality limits; the corresponding loads $Q_0Tg_E$ are retained and reported separately. Let $J_{\mathrm{des/op}}$ contain the declared volume, aeration, pumping, and wasted-solids contributions. The complete engineering score is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:network_objective}
J(\vartheta,\chi)=\lambda_QJ_Q(\chi)+J_{\mathrm{des/op}}(\vartheta,\chi),
\qquad \lambda_Q>0.
\end{equation}

Equipment and permit limits answer whether a candidate may be operated; they should not move the predicted state merely to manufacture compliance. They are therefore checked after Equation~\eqref{eq:network_lower_qp}. Denote them by $g_{\mathrm{cap}}(\vartheta,\chi)\leq0$. Whole-plant SRT is one such quantity. Because the Clarifier inventory is derived from the layer profile, it is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:srt}
\mathrm{SRT}(\vartheta,\chi)=
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}V_it_X^\top c_i+
\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_\ell}V_{\mathrm{cl},\ell}s_\ell}
{Q_0\left(q_Et_X^\top c_E+wt_X^\top c_U\right)}.
\end{equation}
The numerator is all suspended solids held in the CSTRs and Clarifier. The denominator contains only solids that cross the plant boundary in effluent and WAS. MLR and RAS are absent because they circulate internally. The SRT is defined only when
\begin{equation}
q_Et_X^\top c_E+wt_X^\top c_U>
10^{-10}\max(1,q_Ct_X^\top c_N);
\end{equation}
otherwise the candidate is rejected instead of being assigned an artificial infinite SRT.

The nested optimization is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:complete_network_bilevel}
\begin{aligned}
\vartheta^*={}&
\argminop_{\vartheta}\ J[\vartheta,\widehat\chi(\vartheta;x)]\\
\text{subject to}\quad
&\vartheta\in\mathcal V,\\
&g_{\mathrm{cap}}[\vartheta,\widehat\chi(\vartheta;x)]\leq0,\\
&\widehat\chi(\vartheta;x)\ \text{solves Equation~\eqref{eq:network_lower_qp}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The lower answer is unique for each proposed $\vartheta$. Across proposals, the quadratic surrogate, flow-dependent matrices, recovered concentrations, and changing active inequalities make the upper response nonlinear, generally nonsmooth, and potentially nonconvex.

\subsection{Bounded Search and the Meaning of the Reported Answer}

Let $n_\vartheta$ be the case-study decision dimension and $D_\vartheta=\diag(\vartheta^U-\vartheta^L)$. The outer algorithm works in the unit box through
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:network_normalization}
z=D_\vartheta^{-1}(\vartheta-\vartheta^L)\in[0,1]^{n_\vartheta}.
\end{equation}
For each $z$, the evaluator reconstructs physical decisions, forms the raw state, solves one QP, independently verifies Equation~\eqref{eq:network_guarantee}, checks capacity constraints, and only then assigns an objective. A failed or infeasible state is recorded and never replaced by the influent, a clipped prediction, or the preceding successful point.

The unit-box center and corners, deterministic full-box and coordinate-face DIRECT exploration (Jones et al., 1993), and bounded multi-basin pattern refinement provide global coverage and explicit boundary checks. Failed and infeasible evaluations receive a finite feasibility-first search merit defined in the methodology. A finite budget does not certify a global optimum; the reported answer is the best verified incumbent at the stated evaluation budget and spatial resolution. The mechanistic reference is interpreted with the same limitation.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{End-to-End Study Design}

The computational study follows four information-separated phases. First, the coupled ASM2d-TSN--Clarifier model generates one fixed 20,000-state design. Second, the fixed estimator is fitted on 16,000 development rows and evaluated once against 4,000 rows held out from that fit using predeclared numerical, predictive, and correction-trust criteria. Third, after that assessment is sealed, the unchanged estimator is refitted on all 20,000 rows and frozen for nominal and robustness optimization. Fourth, the complete mechanistic network is evaluated at every selected point and searched independently. No assessment, nominal, or robustness response alters the mechanistic domain, estimator, features, objective weights, trust limits, or numerical thresholds.

\subsection{Coupled Reactor--Clarifier Data Generator}

The reaction model is ASM2d with two-step nitrification (ASM2d-TSN), comprising $P=28$ processes and $F=20$ components (Guerrero et al., 2011; Henze et al., 2006). Appendix~\ref{app:mechanistic_definition} gives every rate, stoichiometric coefficient, composition coefficient, and parameter value used here. Every matrix, prediction, and physical check uses the fixed order
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:component_order}
\begin{split}
(&S_O,S_F,S_A,S_{NH4},S_{NO2},S_{NO3},S_{N2},S_{PO4},S_I,S_{ALK},\\
&X_I,X_S,X_H,X_{PAO},X_{PP},X_{PHA},X_{AOB},X_{NOB},X_{MeP},X_{MeOH}).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The first ten entries are soluble and the last ten particulate. Component units remain those of the mechanistic model; COD, TN, TP, and TSS are calculated only after a complete component state is available through the fixed $I_{\mathrm{comp}}$ matrix.

The case study fixes $N=5$ equal-volume CSTRs. CSTRs 1--2 are unaerated denitrification stages, CSTRs 3--5 share one manipulated aerobic intensity, and MLR is taken from CSTR 5. Appendix~\ref{app:stoichiometry} defines a deterministic five-row physical conservation matrix $B$ and its row-normalized form $A$. For a matrix $C\in\R^{m\times n}$ with singular values $\sigma_1\geq\sigma_2\geq\cdots$, define $\operatorname{rank}_\varepsilon(C)$ as the number satisfying $\sigma_k>\max(m,n)\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}\sigma_1$. Generation stops unless $\operatorname{rank}_\varepsilon(\nu)=15$, $\operatorname{rank}_\varepsilon(B)=\operatorname{rank}_\varepsilon(A)=5$, and $\|A\nu^\top\|_\infty$, $\|Ae_{S_O}\|_\infty$, and $\max_k|\|A_{k,:}\|_2-1|$ are each no larger than $10^{-10}$.

The last CSTR feeds a non-reactive, one-dimensional $N_\ell=10$ layer Clarifier based on the flux-limited clarification--thickening model of Tak\'acs et al. (1991) and the benchmark conventions of Alex et al. (2018) and Jeppsson et al. (2007). To make the numerical generator reproducible, let $s_\ell$ be layer TSS, $s_F=t_X^\top c_5$ the feed TSS, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:settling_velocity}
v_s(s)=\max\!\left\{0,\min\!\left[v_0^{\max},
v_0\left(e^{-r_h(s-s_{\min})}-e^{-r_p(s-s_{\min})}\right)\right]\right\},
\qquad s_{\min}=f_{ns}s_F.
\end{equation}
The two exponentials describe hindered settling at high solids and the low-concentration branch; the outer bounds prevent a negative settling speed or a speed above $v_0^{\max}$. Define the unrestricted gravity flux $j_s(s)=v_s(s)s$. At the interface between top-to-bottom layers $\ell$ and $\ell+1$, the benchmark receiver-capacity limiter is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clarifier_flux_limiter}
j^{\mathrm{set}}_{\ell+1/2}=
\begin{cases}
j_s(s_\ell), & s_{\ell+1}\leq X_t,\\
\min\{j_s(s_\ell),j_s(s_{\ell+1})\}, & s_{\ell+1}>X_t.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
This expression lets dilute receiving layers accept the incoming gravity flux and limits that flux when the receiving layer is already concentrated.

Take downward flux as positive and let the feed enter layer $f=5$ in a top-to-bottom, one-based array (the fifth layer from the top and sixth from the bottom). Above the feed, bulk liquid moves upward at $v_E=Q_0q_E/A_{\mathrm{cl}}$; below it, bulk liquid moves downward at $v_U=Q_0q_U/A_{\mathrm{cl}}$. The total internal-interface TSS flux is therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clarifier_total_flux}
J_{\ell+1/2}=
\begin{cases}
-v_Es_{\ell+1}+j^{\mathrm{set}}_{\ell+1/2}, & \ell<f,\\
v_Us_\ell+j^{\mathrm{set}}_{\ell+1/2}, & \ell\geq f.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
At the water surface and hopper, $J_{1/2}=-v_Es_1$ and $J_{N_\ell+1/2}=v_Us_{N_\ell}$; no gravity flux is allowed to cross those physical boundaries. The finite-volume layer balance is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clarifier_layer_balance}
V_{\mathrm{cl},\ell}\frac{ds_\ell}{dt}
=A_{\mathrm{cl}}(J_{\ell-1/2}-J_{\ell+1/2})
\,+\,\mathbf1_{\{\ell=f\}}Q_0q_Cs_F.
\end{equation}
Summing Equation~\eqref{eq:clarifier_layer_balance} over all layers cancels internal fluxes and recovers $q_Cs_F=q_Es_1+q_Us_{N_\ell}$ at steady state. This is why the endpoint equations in the lower QP are not arbitrary additions.

Soluble components follow the liquid phase unchanged. Particulate components share the TSS flux and retain the Clarifier-feed particulate proportions. The complete layer reconstruction is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clarifier_component_reconstruction}
c_{\mathrm{cl},\ell,j}=
\begin{cases}
c_{5,j},&j\in\mathcal S,\\
(c_{5,j}/s_F)s_\ell,&j\in\mathcal X,\ s_F>0,\\
0,&j\in\mathcal X,\ s_F=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
J_{\ell+1/2,j}=(c_{5,j}/s_F)J_{\ell+1/2}
\quad(j\in\mathcal X, s_F>0).
\end{equation}
Thus $c_{E,j}=(c_{5,j}/s_F)s_1$ and $c_{U,j}=(c_{5,j}/s_F)s_{N_\ell}$ for particulates. Because every particulate entry of $t_X$ is positive, $s_F=0$ in a non-negative state implies that all particulate feed coordinates are zero, so the limiting definition is mass consistent.

The mechanistic dynamic state is $y=(c_1,\ldots,c_5,s_1,\ldots,s_{10})\in\R_+^{110}$. At every residual evaluation, Equation~\eqref{eq:clarifier_component_reconstruction} gives $c_E$ and $c_U$, and Equation~\eqref{eq:headworks_mixing} gives $m$. The transient reactor balance whose steady form is Equation~\eqref{eq:stage_balance} is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reactor_dynamic_balance}
\frac{dc_i}{dt}=d_i(c_{i-1}-c_i)+\nu^\top\varrho(c_i)+\omega_i e_{S_O},
\qquad c_0=m.
\end{equation}
The five vector derivatives in Equation~\eqref{eq:reactor_dynamic_balance} and the ten scalar layer derivatives in Equation~\eqref{eq:clarifier_layer_balance} therefore form one coupled function $\dot y=f(y;\vartheta,x)$. Eliminating the mixer and outlet algebraic variables in this way does not break the loop: a change in the bottom layer immediately changes RAS, the mixer, CSTR 1, and every downstream state in the same residual evaluation.

The nonlinear residual is expressed in dimensionless-state rates. Reactor component $(i,j)$ is divided by $c_j^{\mathrm{ref}}=\max(1,x_j^U)$, where $x_j^U$ is the upper bound in Table~\ref{tab:influent_ranges}; every layer derivative uses one fixed start-specific reference $s_0^{\mathrm{ref}}=\max[1,t_X^\top c_5(y_0)]$. These values form the positive diagonal scale $S_y$. The scaled derivative replay used for steady-state acceptance evaluates the same component references and the current Clarifier-feed TSS reference. This scaling gives one tolerance the same engineering meaning for dissimilar quantities such as dissolved oxygen and sludge-blanket solids.

Two initial states are prescribed. Start 1 uses $c_i=x$ for every stage and $s_\ell=t_X^\top x$ for every layer. Start 2 uses
\begin{equation}
c_i=E_S^\top E_Sx+\alpha_iE_X^\top E_Xx,
\quad (\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_5)=(1.5,2.0,2.5,3.0,3.5),
\end{equation}
and, after forming $s_F=t_X^\top c_5$,
\begin{equation}
(s_1,\ldots,s_{10})=s_F(0.002,0.005,0.01,0.03,0.10,0.50,1.25,2.25,3.25,4.00).
\end{equation}
Each prescribed initial state $y_0$ is first relaxed in scaled coordinates by integrating
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\widetilde y}{dt}=S_y^{-1}f(S_y\widetilde y;\vartheta,x),
\qquad \widetilde y(0)=S_y^{-1}y_0,
\end{equation}
with variable-order BDF. The relaxation horizon follows the slow solids-removal mode,
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{rel}}=\max(400,40/w)\ \mathrm{d},
\end{equation}
and the adaptive maximum step is $T_{\mathrm{rel}}/100$. The factor $40/w$ provides a conservative horizon as wasting approaches its lower bound; it is an upper limit, not a forced simulation duration. Integration terminates early when the scaled derivative infinity norm reaches $10^{-9}$ d$^{-1}$. Relative and scaled-coordinate absolute tolerances are $10^{-7}$ and $10^{-9}$.

A successful integrator status is not by itself a positivity certificate. Every stored physical state on the scaled-state trajectory is checked and must be strictly positive. If an internal evaluation fails or any stored state is non-positive, the same start is retried with
\begin{equation}
z=\log\widetilde y,
\qquad
\frac{dz}{dt}=\diag(e^z)^{-1}S_y^{-1}f(S_ye^z;\vartheta,x),
\end{equation}
which keeps every evaluated physical state on that route positive without clipping. The relaxed endpoint is replayed against the complete $10^{-8}$ acceptance contract below. Only an endpoint that misses that contract is supplied to a non-negative bounded trust-region-reflective least-squares calculation as a local polish, using \texttt{xtol}=\texttt{ftol}=\texttt{gtol}=$10^{-9}$ and at most 5000 residual evaluations. Start 1 fixes the branch when this complete route is accepted; Start 2 is used only after the complete Start-1 route fails. No preceding Latin-hypercube solution initializes another row.

For the stability screen, the reduced Jacobian is $J_y=\partial f/\partial y$ after the algebraic quantities have been eliminated. Column $j$ is calculated by a centered finite difference with step $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}}\max(1,|y_j|)$, using a forward difference only when the centered step would cross zero. The deployment QP's uniqueness is not interpreted as uniqueness of this nonlinear steady state; the prescribed initialization route defines the branch represented in the data.

\subsection{Mechanistic Design and Stored Targets}

A single strength-1 Latin hypercube (McKay et al., 1979) contains exactly $n_D=20{,}000$ points in the 25-dimensional box, ordered as $(H,a,r_I,r_R,w,x_1,\ldots,x_{20})$. Its random stream is defined here so that no software-specific seeding convention is required. All variables in the following generator are unsigned 64-bit integers; $\oplus$ is bitwise exclusive-or, $\gg$ is a logical right shift, and every addition and multiplication is reduced modulo $2^{64}$. Starting from $\mathsf q_0=42$, draw the 64-bit word $\mathcal U_t$ by
\begin{align}
\mathsf q_{t+1}&=\mathsf q_t+\mathtt{9E3779B97F4A7C15}_{16},\\
\mathsf v_1&=[\mathsf q_{t+1}\oplus(\mathsf q_{t+1}\gg30)]\,\mathtt{BF58476D1CE4E5B9}_{16},\\
\mathsf v_2&=[\mathsf v_1\oplus(\mathsf v_1\gg27)]\,\mathtt{94D049BB133111EB}_{16},\\
\mathcal U_t&=\mathsf v_2\oplus(\mathsf v_2\gg31).
\end{align}
The draw counter $t$ advances once for every generated word, including a rejected word. For dimension $j$, initialize $\pi_j=(0,\ldots,n_D-1)$. For the separate shuffle index $k=n_D-1,\ldots,1$, let $b=k+1$ and $L_b=2^{64}-(2^{64}\bmod b)$; draw until the current $\mathcal U_t<L_b$ and swap entries $k$ and $\mathcal U_t\bmod b$. This rejection step gives an unbiased Fisher--Yates permutation. After that permutation is complete, draw once for each row in increasing order and set $\xi_{ij}=(\mathcal U_t\gg11)/2^{53}$. The unit-box coordinate is
\begin{equation}
\zeta_{ij}=\frac{\pi_j(i-1)+\xi_{ij}}{n_D},\qquad i=1,\ldots,n_D.
\end{equation}
Affine range maps send $\zeta_{ij}$ to the physical variables. Dimensions and rows are generated in the displayed order, which fixes random-number consumption. Each point must produce an accepted state under Table~\ref{tab:mechanistic_acceptance}. A failed point is not replaced. If any point remains unresolved after the fixed retry route, the study stops without a fitted response. All start attempts and diagnostics are retained. The robustness design restarts the same generator with the scalar state stated in its subsection.

For each accepted row, the target vector is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mechanistic_target}
\chi_{\mathrm{ASM}}=
(m,c_1,\ldots,c_5,g_E,g_U,s_1,\ldots,s_{10})\in\R^{170}.
\end{equation}
The full component state of every Clarifier layer, $c_E$, $c_U$, component and TSS recoveries, composites, loadings, SRT, reaction and oxygen-transfer rates, initialization route, stability result, residuals, and solve time are retained as diagnostics. Outlet mass flows are modeled because their scale remains well behaved when $q_U$ is small; the layer TSS values make $M_{\mathrm{cl}}$ a derived quantity through Equation~\eqref{eq:clarifier_inventory}.

The 20,000 design rows retain their immutable Latin-hypercube generation order. Rows 1--16,000 form the development set $\mathcal D$, and rows 16,001--20,000 form the predictive-assessment set $\mathcal A$. This membership is fixed before any response is examined. The assessment set is held out from the development fit and is used once as a pass--fail evaluation; it does not alter the estimator, features, numerical thresholds, or trust criteria. It assesses the fixed procedure as applied to the development fit. Because these rows subsequently enter the production refit, its metrics are not described as holdout errors of the final 20,000-row coefficient array.

\subsection{Fixed Standardized Multiresponse Least-Squares Estimator}

For a fitting index set $\mathcal I$ containing $n$ rows, define the population mean, population standard deviation, and numerical reference magnitude of a scalar coordinate $v$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fit_coordinate_scale}
\overline v_{\mathcal I}=\frac1n\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}v_i,
\quad
s_{\mathcal I}(v)=\left[\frac1n\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}(v_i-\overline v_{\mathcal I})^2\right]^{1/2},
\quad
a_{\mathcal I}(v)=\max\!\left(1,\max_{i\in\mathcal I}|v_i|\right).
\end{equation}
A coordinate is numerically variable only when
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nonzero_variance_rule}
s_{\mathcal I}(v)>10^{-12}a_{\mathcal I}(v).
\end{equation}
This rule is applied to every design coordinate, influent coordinate, nonconstant monomial, and target coordinate. All 350 nonconstant feature coordinates and all 170 target coordinates must pass; the leading constant feature is retained by definition. Otherwise the fit is invalid.

Using only rows in $\mathcal I$, construct all centers and scales in Equations~\eqref{eq:standardized_inputs_states}--\eqref{eq:system_feature_map}. Let $Y_{\mathcal I}$ contain the mechanistic targets from Equation~\eqref{eq:mechanistic_target}, in the same row order as the features. The fitting matrices are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:training_matrices}
\Phi_{\mathcal I}=\begin{bmatrix}\phi_1^\top\\\vdots\\\phi_n^\top\end{bmatrix}\in\R^{n\times d_\phi},
\qquad
\widetilde Y_{\mathcal I}=(Y_{\mathcal I}-\mathbf1_n\mu_\chi^\top)D_\chi^{-1}\in\R^{n\times L},
\end{equation}
where $d_\phi=351$ and $L=170$. The fixed estimator is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:training_objective}
\widehat C_{\mathcal I}=\argminop_{C\in\R^{L\times d_\phi}}
\frac{1}{nL}\left\|\widetilde Y_{\mathcal I}-\Phi_{\mathcal I}C^\top\right\|_F^2.
\end{equation}
Every output is estimated from the single fixed standardized design in Equation~\eqref{eq:training_matrices}.

A thin singular-value decomposition
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design_svd}
\Phi_{\mathcal I}=U\Sigma V^\top,
\qquad \sigma_1\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{d_\phi}\geq0,
\end{equation}
defines
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design_rank_condition}
\tau_{\mathrm{rank}}=\max(n,d_\phi)\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}\sigma_1,
\qquad
\kappa_2(\Phi_{\mathcal I})=\frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_{d_\phi}}.
\end{equation}
The design is admissible only when
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:design_acceptance}
\sigma_{d_\phi}>\tau_{\mathrm{rank}},
\qquad
\kappa_2(\Phi_{\mathcal I})\leq10^8.
\end{equation}
Under these conditions Equation~\eqref{eq:training_objective} has one unique least-squares minimizer supported by the observed design.

The coefficients are calculated by column-pivoted Householder QR,
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\mathcal I}\Pi=QR_\Phi,
\qquad
\widehat C_{\mathcal I}^{\top}=W_{\mathrm{QR}}
=\Pi R_\Phi^{-1}Q^\top\widetilde Y_{\mathcal I}.
\end{equation}
The triangular solution is followed by one iterative-refinement replay in the same factorization. With $R_Y^{(0)}=\Phi_{\mathcal I}W_{\mathrm{QR}}-\widetilde Y_{\mathcal I}$, solve
\begin{equation}
\Delta W=\Pi R_\Phi^{-1}Q^\top(-R_Y^{(0)}),
\qquad W_{\mathrm{QR}}\leftarrow W_{\mathrm{QR}}+\Delta W.
\end{equation}
This correction does not change the least-squares estimator; it suppresses triangular-solve roundoff before the normal-equation audit, which is especially important when a response is reproduced nearly exactly.
The independent SVD calculation is
\begin{equation}
W_{\mathrm{SVD}}=V\Sigma^{-1}U^\top\widetilde Y_{\mathcal I}.
\end{equation}
With $R_Y=\Phi_{\mathcal I}W_{\mathrm{QR}}-\widetilde Y_{\mathcal I}$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:coefficient_acceptance}
\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}=
\frac{\|\Phi_{\mathcal I}^\top R_Y\|_F}
{\max\{1,\|\Phi_{\mathcal I}\|_2\|R_Y\|_F\}},
\quad
\eta_{\mathrm{agree}}=
\frac{\|W_{\mathrm{QR}}-W_{\mathrm{SVD}}\|_F}
{\max(1,\|W_{\mathrm{SVD}}\|_F)},
\quad
\max(\eta_{\mathrm{opt}},\eta_{\mathrm{agree}})
\leq100\kappa_2(\Phi_{\mathcal I})\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}.
\end{equation}
All coefficients must also be finite. Failure of Equations~\eqref{eq:nonzero_variance_rule}, \eqref{eq:design_acceptance}, or \eqref{eq:coefficient_acceptance} invalidates the response; no alternate estimator is substituted.

The equality and inequality scales are calculated from physical terms, not from the nearly zero residuals of accepted mechanistic states. For fitting row $i$, define the signed equality term list $\mathcal T^{(b)}_{ir}$ as follows: mixer component $j$ uses $\{q_Pm_j,-x_j,-r_Ic_{N,j},-(r_R/q_U)g_{U,j}\}$; the first invariant block uses $\{(Ac_1)_k,-(Am)_k\}$; invariant block $h\geq2$ uses $\{(Ac_h)_k,-(Ac_{h-1})_k\}$; Clarifier component $j$ uses $\{g_{E,j},g_{U,j},-q_Cc_{N,j}\}$; soluble component $j$ uses $\{(E_Sg_U)_j,-q_U(E_Sc_N)_j\}$; and the endpoint rows use $\{q_Es_1,-t_X^\top g_E\}$ and $\{q_Us_{N_\ell},-t_X^\top g_U\}$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:balance_scale}
(D_b)_{rr}=\max\!\left\{10^{-12},
\left[\frac1n\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\frac1{|\mathcal T^{(b)}_{ir}|}
\sum_{t\in\mathcal T^{(b)}_{ir}}t^2\right]^{1/2}\right\}.
\end{equation}
For inequality row $r$, the term lists are $\{q_U(E_Xc_N)_j,-(E_Xg_U)_j\}$, $\{s_1,-s_\ell\}$, or $\{s_\ell,-s_{N_\ell}\}$ in the row order of Equation~\eqref{eq:network_inequality_operator}, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:inequality_scale}
(D_g)_{rr}=\max\!\left\{10^{-12},
\left[\frac1n\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\frac1{|\mathcal T^{(g)}_{ir}|}
\sum_{t\in\mathcal T^{(g)}_{ir}}t^2\right]^{1/2}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The grouping and row order in Equations~\eqref{eq:balance_scale}--\eqref{eq:inequality_scale} are part of the deployed-response and lower-QP definition.

\subsection{One-Time Predictive Assessment and Production Refit}

The development response uses $\mathcal I=\mathcal D$. Its centers, scales, coefficient matrix, and row scales remain fixed while all 4,000 assessment rows are evaluated. For assessment row $i$, coordinate $j$, and prediction form $h\in\{\mathrm{raw},\mathrm{aff},\mathrm{dep}\}$, let $e_{ij}^{(h)}=\widehat y_{ij}^{(h)}-Y_{ij}$. With $n_A=4000$,
\begin{align}
\mathrm{RMSE}_j^{(h)}&=\left[\frac1{n_A}\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}(e_{ij}^{(h)})^2\right]^{1/2},
&\mathrm{MAE}_j^{(h)}&=\frac1{n_A}\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}|e_{ij}^{(h)}|,\\
\mathrm{Bias}_j^{(h)}&=\frac1{n_A}\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}e_{ij}^{(h)},
&\mathrm{nRMSE}_j^{(h)}&=\frac{\mathrm{RMSE}_j^{(h)}}{s_{\chi,j}},\\
R_j^{2,(h)}&=1-\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}(e_{ij}^{(h)})^2}
{\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}(Y_{ij}-\overline Y_{\mathcal A,j})^2}.
\end{align}
For the $R^2$ denominator, Equation~\eqref{eq:nonzero_variance_rule} is applied to target coordinate $j$ with $\mathcal I=\mathcal A$; a failing denominator is reported as undefined. For a coordinate block $\mathcal B$, the standardized block error is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:assessment_block_error}
E_{\mathcal B}^{(h)}=
\left[\frac1{n_A|\mathcal B|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}\sum_{j\in\mathcal B}
\left(\frac{e_{ij}^{(h)}}{s_{\chi,j}}\right)^2\right]^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
It is reported for the mixer, every CSTR, both outlet-flow blocks, the layer profile, and the complete state. Overflow and underflow COD, TN, TP, and TSS and normalized Clarifier inventory use the same RMSE, MAE, bias, nRMSE, and $R^2$ definitions. Each derived-response standard deviation is calculated on $\mathcal D$ and must pass Equation~\eqref{eq:nonzero_variance_rule} before it is used as an nRMSE denominator; failure invalidates the assessment.

The assessment also quantifies reliance on projection. Let
\begin{equation}
e_i^{\mathrm{raw}}=D_\chi^{-1}(\chi_{\mathrm{raw},i}-Y_i),
\quad e_i^{\mathrm{dep}}=D_\chi^{-1}(\widehat\chi_i-Y_i),
\quad u_i=D_\chi^{-1}(\widehat\chi_i-\chi_{\mathrm{raw},i}),
\quad \kappa_{\mathrm{corr},i}=\frac{\|u_i\|_2}{\sqrt L}.
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:correction_trust_metrics}
E_{\mathrm{state}}^{\mathrm{dep}}=
\left[\frac1{n_AL}\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}\|e_i^{\mathrm{dep}}\|_2^2\right]^{1/2},
\qquad
\rho_{\mathrm{corr}}=
\left[\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}\|u_i\|_2^2}
{\max(10^{-24},\sum_{i\in\mathcal A}\|e_i^{\mathrm{raw}}\|_2^2)}\right]^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
If $\kappa_{\mathrm{corr},(1)}\leq\cdots\leq\kappa_{\mathrm{corr},(n_A)}$ are ordered, then $Q_{0.95}(\kappa_{\mathrm{corr}})=\kappa_{\mathrm{corr},(\lceil0.95n_A\rceil)}$.

The development response is admissible only when all 4,000 deployed QPs pass Equation~\eqref{eq:lower_acceptance}, $E_{\mathrm{state}}^{\mathrm{dep}}<1$, $\rho_{\mathrm{corr}}\leq0.50$, $Q_{0.95}(\kappa_{\mathrm{corr}})\leq0.50$, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:assessment_trust_gate}
\|e_i^{\mathrm{dep}}\|_2^2\leq\|e_i^{\mathrm{raw}}\|_2^2
+10^{-6}\left(1+\|e_i^{\mathrm{raw}}\|_2^2\right),
\qquad i\in\mathcal A.
\end{equation}
The final inequality checks the non-expansive geometry of projection relative to an accepted mechanistic state. Failure of any assessment condition ends the study; assessment responses do not alter the estimator.

After the assessment record is sealed, the production response is refitted on all 20,000 rows using the unchanged equations. Every center, scale, row scale, QR factorization, SVD check, and coefficient is recomputed. The four mechanistic overflow composite coordinates used in $D_T$ must each pass Equation~\eqref{eq:nonzero_variance_rule} on the 20,000 production rows before their population standard deviations are inverted. The production response is frozen only after Equations~\eqref{eq:nonzero_variance_rule}, \eqref{eq:design_acceptance}, and \eqref{eq:coefficient_acceptance} pass and all 20,000 production-row deployments pass Equation~\eqref{eq:lower_acceptance}.

\subsection{Nominal Objective and Constraints}

For Equation~\eqref{eq:nominal_influent}, set $T=I_{\mathrm{comp}}$ and $\alpha_Q=\tfrac14\mathbf1_4$. Let $D_T$ contain production-fit standard deviations of overflow COD, TN, TP, and TSS. The primary objective is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:case_objective}
\begin{split}
J={}&0.50\,\alpha_Q^\top D_T^{-1}I_{\mathrm{comp}}c_E
+0.15\,\frac{H-H^L}{H^U-H^L}
+0.20\,a\\
&+0.05\,\frac{r_I-r_I^L}{r_I^U-r_I^L}
+0.05\,\frac{r_R-r_R^L}{r_R^U-r_R^L}
+0.05\,\frac{w\,t_X^\top c_U}{w^U X_U^{\max}}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The final term is proportional to the wasted-solids mass rate $Q_0w\,t_X^\top c_U$; penalizing $w$ alone would treat a dilute and a concentrated waste stream as equally costly. The constant $Q_0$ cancels in the normalization. All six coefficients are declared before optimization and sum to one. They define a transparent dimensionless case, not a claim of universal economic equivalence. Sensitivity runs change the five non-quality coefficients while retaining the frozen surrogate and original normalization constants.

The production design also defines a leverage support screen. With $\Phi_P$ denoting the 20,000-row production feature matrix,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:leverage_screen}
\ell(\vartheta,x)=\phi(\vartheta,x)^\top(\Phi_P^\top\Phi_P)^{-1}\phi(\vartheta,x),
\qquad
\ell_{\max}=\max_{i=1,\ldots,20000}\ell(\vartheta_i,x_i).
\end{equation}
The displayed inverse defines the scalar; triangular solves with the production QR factor are used numerically.

Three further quantities guard the physical relaxation. The correction magnitude is $\kappa_{\mathrm{corr}}=\|\widehat u\|_2/\sqrt L$. For particulate coordinates whose feed mass exceeds $10^{-10}q_C\max(1,|\mu_{c_N,j}|+s_{c_N,j})$, let $\Delta_\eta=\max_j\eta_j-\min_j\eta_j$; set $\Delta_\eta=0$ if fewer than two ratios are available. Finally, calculate the layer fluxes in Equations~\eqref{eq:clarifier_flux_limiter}--\eqref{eq:clarifier_total_flux} from the deployed profile and define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clarifier_flux_screen}
r_{\mathrm{flux}}=
\frac{\displaystyle\max_\ell\left|
A_{\mathrm{cl}}(J_{\ell-1/2}-J_{\ell+1/2})
+\mathbf1_{\{\ell=f\}}Q_0q_Cs_F\right|}
{Q_0\max(q_Cs_F,1)}.
\end{equation}
Thus a candidate cannot obtain a favorable SRT by relying on a large projection, unsupported polynomial leverage, component-selective particulate capture, or a layer profile that strongly violates the nonlinear Clarifier flux equation.

No additional discharge constraint is active in the primary case; COD, TN, TP, and TSS are represented in the objective. The complete dimensionless upper-constraint vector is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:complete_capacity_vector}
\widetilde g_{\mathrm{cap}}=
\begin{bmatrix}
(8-\mathrm{SRT})/8\\
(\mathrm{SRT}-30)/30\\
(\mathrm{SOR}-20)/20\\
(\mathrm{SLR}-100)/100\\
(t_X^\top c_U-15000)/15000\\
(\kappa_{\mathrm{corr}}-0.50)/0.50\\
(\ell-\ell_{\max})/\max(\ell_{\max},10^{-12})\\
(\Delta_\eta-0.05)/0.05\\
(r_{\mathrm{flux}}-0.05)/0.05
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Here the dimensional limits use days, m d$^{-1}$, kg TSS m$^{-2}$ d$^{-1}$, and g TSS m$^{-3}$ as appropriate. A successfully deployed candidate is upper-feasible when $\|[\widetilde g_{\mathrm{cap}}]_+\|_\infty\leq10^{-8}$. These requirements are checked after the lower QP and therefore cannot move its state toward compliance.

\subsection{Lower-Level Numerical Acceptance}

For a fixed candidate, put Equation~\eqref{eq:network_lower_qp} in the standard scaled form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:standard_lower_qp}
\mathsf E=D_b^{-1}\mathcal H D_\chi,
\quad e=D_b^{-1}(b-\mathcal H\chi_{\mathrm{raw}}),
\quad
\mathsf I=\begin{bmatrix}-I_L\\D_g^{-1}\mathcal G D_\chi\end{bmatrix},
\quad
h_{\mathrm{QP}}=\begin{bmatrix}D_\chi^{-1}\chi_{\mathrm{raw}}\\-D_g^{-1}\mathcal G\chi_{\mathrm{raw}}\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Thus $\widehat u$ minimizes $\tfrac12u^\top u$ subject to $\mathsf Eu=e$ and $\mathsf Iu\leq h_{\mathrm{QP}}$. If $\sigma_1\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_R$ are the singular values of $\mathsf E$, the candidate is rejected unless
\begin{equation}
\sigma_R>\max(R,L)\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}\sigma_1.
\end{equation}

Let $\lambda_E\in\R^R$ be the equality multiplier, $\lambda_I\in\R_+^{L+R_g}$ the inequality multiplier, and $\varsigma=h_{\mathrm{QP}}-\mathsf I\widehat u$ the inequality slack. The independently recomputed KKT relations are
\begin{equation}
\mathsf E\widehat u=e,\quad \varsigma\geq0,\quad \lambda_I\geq0,\quad
\widehat u+\mathsf E^\top\lambda_E+\mathsf I^\top\lambda_I=0,
\quad \lambda_I\odot\varsigma=0.
\end{equation}
Their dimensionless residuals are
\begin{align}
r_E&=\|\mathsf E\widehat u-e\|_\infty,
&r_G&=\|[D_g^{-1}\mathcal G\widehat\chi]_+\|_\infty,\\
r_+&=\|[-D_\chi^{-1}\widehat\chi]_+\|_\infty,
&r_{\mathrm{dual}}&=\frac{\|[-\lambda_I]_+\|_\infty}{1+\|\lambda_I\|_\infty},\\
r_{\mathrm{stat}}&=
\frac{\|\widehat u+\mathsf E^\top\lambda_E+\mathsf I^\top\lambda_I\|_\infty}
{1+\|\widehat u\|_\infty+\|\mathsf E^\top\lambda_E\|_\infty+\|\mathsf I^\top\lambda_I\|_\infty},
&r_{\mathrm{comp}}&=
\frac{\|\lambda_I\odot\varsigma\|_\infty}
{1+\|\lambda_I\|_\infty\|\varsigma\|_\infty}.
\end{align}
Acceptance requires finite primal and dual arrays and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lower_acceptance}
r_E,r_G,r_{\mathrm{stat}},r_{\mathrm{dual}},r_{\mathrm{comp}}\leq10^{-8},
\qquad r_+\leq10^{-10}.
\end{equation}
Multipliers in these equations correspond to the displayed scaled matrices regardless of internal solver scaling. The QP uses double precision, absolute and relative tolerances $10^{-8}$, polishing, and at most 100,000 iterations (Stellato et al., 2018). Any solver status is provisional until Equation~\eqref{eq:lower_acceptance} passes. Every assessment, production, timing, DIRECT, face, pattern, and incumbent-replay QP starts from zero primal and dual arrays. This all-cold rule costs more factorization work than a lineage-dependent warm start, but it removes execution-order and parent-selection ambiguity from the scientific result. If the first solve fails acceptance, one cold retry reconstructs the factorization and again starts all arrays at zero; failure of that retry invalidates the candidate. The final incumbent is evaluated once more by an independently constructed cold QP. The plant-boundary identity, Clarifier balances, inventory, and recovered concentrations are also replayed from the unrounded state; no raw, affine, clipped, influent, or preceding state is substituted.

\subsection{Outer Search and Mechanistic Validation}

The first five entries of Equation~\eqref{eq:complete_capacity_vector} are engineering constraints; the final four are surrogate-trust constraints. For a successfully deployed surrogate state, let
\begin{equation}
V_{\mathrm{surr}}=\|[\widetilde g_{\mathrm{cap}}]_+\|_\infty.
\end{equation}
The finite scalar supplied to the search algorithm is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:search_merit}
F_{\mathrm{search}}=
\begin{cases}
J/(1+J), & \text{accepted lower state and }V_{\mathrm{surr}}\leq10^{-8},\\
1+V_{\mathrm{surr}}/(1+V_{\mathrm{surr}}), & \text{accepted lower state and }V_{\mathrm{surr}}>10^{-8},\\
2, & \text{failed, non-finite, or undefined candidate}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Because $J\geq0$, every feasible point ranks ahead of every infeasible point, every evaluated infeasible point ranks ahead of a failed point, and ordering by $J$ is unchanged among feasible points. The displayed transforms are part of the optimizer definition: their numerical spacing, not only their ordering, determines the DIRECT lower hull. A failed lower solve, invalid SRT denominator, or non-finite response consumes one distinct-evaluation unit. Candidate keys are the exact binary representations of the normalized coordinates; a cached duplicate consumes no additional unit.

The five controls are normalized to $[0,1]^5$. The center initializes the full-box DIRECT partition, and all 32 corners are evaluated as separate boundary probes. Corner and later face evaluations are eligible incumbents but do not alter the full-box DIRECT hull. For rectangle $r$, let $c_r$ be its center, $F_r=F_{\mathrm{search}}(c_r)$, and $d_r$ the Euclidean distance from its center to a vertex. With $F_{\min}$ the smallest merit among centers in the current partition, rectangle $r$ is potentially optimal when some $K_r\geq0$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
F_r-K_rd_r\leq F_j-K_rd_j\quad\text{for every rectangle }j,
\qquad
F_r-K_rd_r\leq F_{\min}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{DIR}}|F_{\min}|,
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon_{\mathrm{DIR}}=10^{-4}$. These inequalities are tested from the lower convex hull of the distinct $(d_r,F_r)$ pairs. A selected rectangle is trisected along every longest coordinate. If its longest side is $L_r$, the new points are $c_r\pm(L_r/3)e_j$ for those coordinates; coordinates are split in increasing order of the smaller merit at their two trial points, with coordinate index breaking ties. For an exact binary64 tie group with the same $(d_r,F_r)$, at most one member is split per iteration; iteration number cycles through the members in lexicographic lower-corner order to avoid a fixed spatial bias on flat failure plateaus. Trial batches are evaluated synchronously and committed in lexicographic coordinate order.

The surrogate search has 25,000 distinct-attempt units. The full-box partition contributes at most 18,000 globally new keys, including its center but excluding the 32 separately counted corners, or stops when every rectangle has maximum side length at most $1/1024$. Next, ten four-dimensional DIRECT partitions search the coordinate faces in the order $z_1=0,z_1=1,\ldots,z_5=0,z_5=1$. Each face contributes at most 300 globally new keys under the same rule and resolution; cached points are free, and a face's hull uses only centers from that face partition. Thus at least 3968 attempts, plus any unused DIRECT quota, remain for local work.

Local seeds are chosen by increasing $F_{\mathrm{search}}$. The best seed is retained, and up to three further seeds are selected greedily subject to $\ell_\infty$ separation of at least $1/8$; if necessary, that threshold is halved until four seeds or all distinct basins are obtained. Each seed starts with mesh $\Delta=1/16$. In cyclic seed order, a complete poll evaluates the axial directions $\{\pm e_j\}$ first and then, lexicographically, all pairwise directions $\{\pm e_j\pm e_k:j<k\}$. Out-of-box coordinates are clipped and duplicates removed. The smallest-merit poll point is accepted if it strictly improves the center; otherwise that seed's mesh is halved. A seed terminates after an unsuccessful poll at $\Delta=1/512$. A split or poll begins only when all of its uncached trial points fit the phase and total budgets. The final feasible incumbent is recomputed by a cold lower solve. If no feasible point is found, the outcome is ``no feasible incumbent''; the smallest violation is reported, but no selected decision or finite regret is defined. This is a finite-resolution refinement, not a stationarity or global-optimality certificate.

For the nominal case and each validation case, the complete mechanistic model is next evaluated at the selected decisions without refitting. Four outcomes are distinguished. If the surrogate search has no feasible incumbent, the case is a surrogate-optimization failure. If the fixed mechanistic solve does not return an accepted, stable steady state, the case is a mechanistic-evaluation failure. If an accepted mechanistic state violates any of the five engineering constraints, the case is a decision-feasibility failure. None of these three outcomes has an ordinary finite regret. If the selected point is accepted and engineering-feasible, it is inserted first into the mechanistic archive and counts against the reference budget.

The mechanistic reference uses the same search merit with $V_{\mathrm{ASM}}$ formed from only the five engineering constraints. Its nominal budget is 10,000 distinct attempts and each robustness budget is 2500. Before DIRECT begins, the selected point and the clipped, deduplicated 50-direction pattern stencil at step $2^{-7}$ are evaluated in center, axial, then lexicographic pairwise order. Corners not already cached are evaluated next. In the nominal reference, the full-box DIRECT partition contributes at most 7000 globally new keys and each coordinate face at most 100; in a robustness reference, the corresponding caps are 1700 and 25. Full-box, corner, stencil, face, failed, and local attempts all count against the stated total. Every remaining unit is assigned to the same multi-basin pattern rule, using up to four nominal seeds and one robustness seed. Even when every preceding quota is filled without a duplicate, at least 1917 nominal units and 467 robustness units remain for local refinement. Every failed mechanistic attempt consumes one unit. If $\widehat\vartheta_s$ is an accepted engineering-feasible surrogate decision and $\vartheta_{\mathrm{ASM},s}^*$ is the best verified mechanistic incumbent, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:regret}
\mathcal R_s=
J[\widehat\vartheta_s,\chi_{\mathrm{ASM}}(\widehat\vartheta_s;x_s)]
-J[\vartheta_{\mathrm{ASM},s}^*,\chi_{\mathrm{ASM}}(\vartheta_{\mathrm{ASM},s}^*;x_s)].
\end{equation}
Because the archive already contains the feasible selected point, its best objective cannot be larger than that point's objective. Hence $\mathcal R_s\geq0$ up to roundoff. A value below $-10^{-8}$ indicates inconsistent objectives, constraints, or archive insertion and invalidates the case. The reported quantity is a gap to a finite-budget incumbent and is not a certificate relative to an unknown global optimum.

\section{Results and Discussion}

Three properties follow directly from the formulation. First, the flow definitions close exactly and lead to the plant-boundary inventory in Equation~\eqref{eq:plant_boundary_invariant}; internal recycle ratios change internal concentrations without appearing as external generation or loss. Second, Equation~\eqref{eq:network_lower_qp} has a nonempty feasible set and an identity Hessian, so every accepted upper candidate has one deployed state. Third, Equation~\eqref{eq:clarifier_inventory} derives Clarifier inventory from ten corrected layer concentrations.

The regression has a direct numerical interpretation. Its 351 dimensionless monomials form one checked full-column-rank design, and a pivoted QR factorization estimates all 170 responses. An independent SVD verifies rank, condition number, and coefficient agreement. The set held out from the development fit quantifies native and standardized prediction errors and measures reliance on physical correction. Production use begins only after the fixed assessment gate passes.

The QP preserves the stated balances, signs, clarification directions, and layer envelope; it does not enforce all 28 reaction-rate equations or the nonlinear flux limiter. Exact equality of every particulate recovery is bilinear when both feed state and recovery are variables. The leverage, correction, recovery-spread, and flux-residual screens bound this relaxation, while matched mechanistic simulations provide the independent test of kinetics and decision feasibility.

The interpretation of the optimization is similarly bounded. Changing $H$ at fixed $Q_0$ changes tank volume, so the primary problem is joint design and operation. The number of stages is prescribed, not optimized. Finally, boundary-augmented DIRECT and multi-basin pattern refinement return a verified finite-budget incumbent. Prediction error at the selected point and the non-negative incumbent gap in Equation~\eqref{eq:regret} answer different questions and must be reported separately.

The recycling $N$-stage activated sludge system is represented as one mixer--reactor--Clarifier response and one unique scaled physical projection. Inputs, targets, and unique quadratic features are standardized from the applicable fitting rows. A full-rank multiresponse least-squares problem is solved by pivoted QR and checked independently by SVD; a set held out from the development fit gates production use; and the unchanged estimator is refitted on all 20,000 states. The physical guarantees are exact for the declared linear network contract, while nonlinear trust screens and independent ASM2d-TSN--Clarifier evaluation address kinetics, flux behavior, operational feasibility, and decision quality.
